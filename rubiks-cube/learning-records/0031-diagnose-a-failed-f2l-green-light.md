# 0031 — Diagnose a Failed F2L Green Light

**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Lesson:** `0031-diagnose-a-failed-f2l-green-light.html`

## What was taught

- A named U setup can achieve **positional success**—the pair reaches `UFR + UF` (right) or `UFL + UF` (left)—without authorizing an insertion.
- The full F2L green light still requires two independent checks: the corner’s white sticker faces the selected side, and the matching edge at `UF` has a front sticker matching the front centre.
- If either check fails, do **not** use `R U R′` or `L′ U′ L` as an insertion attempt.
- Safe response: stop, preserve the empty working slot, re-read; normalize once through that empty slot when necessary, then re-read. Choosing another unsolved pair is valid.

## Key non-obvious insights

- Correct locations are a positional claim, not an orientation claim. This separates a successful planned setup from a safe insertion.
- A white-up corner cannot be repaired by cycling U: U moves change top-layer positions but not the corner’s white-sticker direction.
- The failure is useful information, not a reason to gamble with a trigger through an otherwise protected workspace.

## Zone of proximal development — next

**F2L: choose the least-disruptive recovery.** Given a failed green light, decide whether the failure is corner orientation, edge readiness, or a positional failure, then choose between one normalization trigger, a new named U setup, or switching to another unsolved pair.
