# 0054 — Collect and Classify a Matching Fresh E/F Pair

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Lesson:** `0054-collect-and-classify-a-matching-fresh-e-f-pair.html`

## What was taught

- After a passed collection plan, Fresh E and Fresh F are collected as separate attempts with one first mismatch recorded at the pre-stated checkpoint.
- The pair is **verdict-ready** only when both attempts retain the same target pair, empty-workspace type, retained cue (including its timing), and checkpoint.
- A newly drifted field does not invalidate the whole drill or explain a miss; it means the drifted attempt must be replaced under the copied condition before a Fresh E/F verdict can be written.

## Key non-obvious insights

- A valid miss under matching conditions is useful evidence. It is not a failed collection or a reason to change the algorithm mid-comparison.
- Classification comes before interpretation: first establish whether Fresh E/F can be compared, then decide whether their first mismatches support retaining, refining, or advancing a drill.
- Replacing only the drifted attempt preserves the tested practice condition and avoids discarding usable matching evidence.
