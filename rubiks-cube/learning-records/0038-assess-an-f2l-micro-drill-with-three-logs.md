# 0038 — Assess an F2L Micro-Drill with Three Logs

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Lesson:** `0038-assess-an-f2l-micro-drill-with-three-logs.html`

## What was taught

- A two-log baseline selects an F2L micro-drill; one fresh third, comparable log assesses whether it helped.
- After five controlled repetitions, retain the drill as improved only when the third attempt’s first mismatch disappears or moves to a later workflow stage.
- If the same earliest mismatch persists, improvement is not yet demonstrated; make the drill more specific or slow down and re-read rather than advancing to a later-stage drill.

## Key non-obvious insights

- A later first mismatch is useful evidence, not a new failure: it shows the former bottleneck no longer arrived first and exposes the next safe target.
- Comparable conditions (same target pair, empty working-slot type, and checkpoints) make the three-log comparison interpretable; changing everything at once does not.
- Destination positions alone remain positional success, not insertion authorization—green-light checks still determine whether a trigger is allowed.

## Zone of proximal development — next

**F2L: turn a three-log assessment into one precise next drill specification.** Given an unchanged or later first mismatch, state the condition, five-rep boundary, observation checkpoint, and retain/change criterion before touching the cube.
