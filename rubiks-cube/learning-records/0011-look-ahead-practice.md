# 0011 — Look-Ahead Practice

**Date:** 2026-07-01
**Lesson:** `0011-look-ahead-practice.html`

## What was taught

- **Look-ahead** defined: using mental bandwidth freed by automatic algorithm execution to plan the next stage while the current one is still running
- The 5 transition windows — exactly when and what to scan at each stage boundary:
  1. White Cross → White Corners: scan top-layer corners while inserting last cross edge
  2. White Corners → 2nd Layer Edges: after flipping, immediately scan top for second-layer edge matching a side centre
  3. 2nd Layer Edges → Yellow Cross: count yellow cross petals on the top face during last edge insertion
  4. Yellow Cross → Sune: count oriented yellow corners (0, 1-adjacent, 1-diagonal) before first Sune
  5. Sune → Corners PLL: during last Sune cycle, glance for headlights positioning
- The Pause Audit method: count full pauses per solve, reduce by 1 per session, drill the longest pause in isolation
- No new algorithms required — this is a purely attentional skill

## Key non-obvious insights

- Algorithms run on a kind of "autopilot" once sufficiently drilled — this is the cognitive resource look-ahead harvests
- A brief glance (< 1 second) is acceptable; the target is eliminating *full stops* where the hands are idle
- The hardest transition for most beginners is White Corners → 2nd Layer because flipping the cube creates a brief disorientation — this is exactly why the flip is also the best scan opportunity
- Counting petals during Stage 3's last move takes practice before it feels natural; beginners should start with just the White Cross → White Corners transition

## Zone of proximal development — next

- **5 full solves with pause audit**: apply all 5 look-ahead windows, record pause count per solve. Confirm a downward trend.
- **Introduction to F2L (First Two Layers)**: once solves are fluent and look-ahead is habitual, collapsing Stages 1–3 into a simultaneous system is the natural CFOP entry point.
- **Sub-3-minute target**: after consistent solves with look-ahead, setting a time target gives structured practice motivation before moving to intermediate methods.
