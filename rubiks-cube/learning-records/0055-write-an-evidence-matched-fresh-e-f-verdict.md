# 0055 — Write an Evidence-Matched Fresh E/F Verdict

**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Lesson:** `0055-write-an-evidence-matched-fresh-e-f-verdict.html`

## What was taught

- A Fresh E/F verdict is written only after the two attempts are verdict-ready: the target pair, empty-workspace type, retained cue including timing, and checkpoint all match.
- The verdict template is: **Because Fresh E and F [shared first-mismatch evidence], [one evidence-matched action].**
- Both clearing a target supports retaining; either attempt repeating it supports a minimal refinement or slower drill; a shared genuinely later mismatch supports one narrow successor drill; condition drift requires replacement evidence instead of a drill decision.

## Key non-obvious insights

- A verdict reports observations and selects a next action; it does not establish a cause. A claim about a bad algorithm or hold is unsupported unless separately tested.
- “Retain and start another drill” is two actions, not a verdict. The later-stage drill becomes the next step only after the current verdict is written.
- Comparable misses are useful: repeated evidence constrains the next practice action more reliably than a vague sense that an attempt went badly.
