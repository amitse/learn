The one concept: a working slot

An F2L working slot is the one unsolved corner-edge slot you deliberately place at front-right. It is the only slot you allow yourself to manipulate while preparing that pair.

Solved pairs stay in the bottom two layers
→
One empty target slot works at front-right
Centres choose the slot; a whole-cube rotation chooses the front. For a white–green–red corner, find the green-red slot, then use a y rotation until that slot is front-right.

The four-move planning loop

Name the pair. Use the Lesson 18 chain: corner → matching edge → centre-defined slot.
Make the target slot your working slot. Hold white down, yellow up; use a whole-cube y rotation so that empty slot is at front-right.
Free the pieces to U if needed. If either partner is trapped incorrectly, eject it with the familiar trigger. Do not sacrifice a solved pair to rescue it.
Use U turns to inspect and set up. U turns move the unsolved top pieces while leaving solved bottom slots intact. When the pair is ready above the working slot, insert with the appropriate right/left trigger from Lesson 12.

This is not a giant F2L case list. It is a safety rail: one empty slot, one identified pair, and setup turns that do not damage progress.

The safety test before a trigger

Stop for one beat before turning R or L. Ask:

Is the target slot at front-right (or its mirror at front-left)?
Are the corner and its exact matching edge available on U?
Will this trigger use the empty working slot—not a completed one?
If the answer to the last question is “no,” rotate the whole cube and reset the working slot. A five-second reset is cheaper than rebuilding a pair you already solved.

One deliberate real-cube rep

Do this once, slowly: after making a cross, choose one F2L corner. Name its edge and slot. Rotate the cube to put that empty slot front-right. If needed, eject its partner; then use only U turns until you are ready to insert. Before the trigger, say: “working slot clear.”

Today’s win: you are no longer turning around whichever pair happens to be visible. You select a pair, create one safe workspace, and preserve what is already solved. Ask your teacher a follow-up question if a setup repeatedly breaks a solved slot.
