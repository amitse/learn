Find an F2L pair without guessing

Your next speed gain is not a new algorithm. Learn one visual skill: read a white corner’s two side colours, then instantly name its matching edge and home slot.

The one rule

An F2L pair is a white corner and the edge that shares its two non-white colours. Those same two colours name the slot between their centres. White tells you it is a corner for the first two layers; it does not identify the edge.

white–blue–red corner
→
blue–red edge → blue–red slot
Say it as a chain: “Blue-red corner. Blue-red edge. Blue-red slot.” The piece colours and the slot name must match exactly.

Use the 3-second scan

Choose a white corner on the U face or in an unsolved slot. Ignore its white sticker.
Read its two side colours. For example, orange + green.
Find the edge with those exact two colours (orange + green), then look between the orange and green centres for its home slot.
Only then solve or eject. If either piece is trapped, use the ejection rule from Lesson 12; do not force a random insertion.

This is recognition, not an algorithm. Once you can name the chain, use the familiar R U R′ or L′ U′ L trigger to insert the pair from the appropriate front slot.

Example: one pair, three names

CornerWhite–orange–blue
Matching edgeOrange–blue
Target slotBetween orange and blue centres

It does not matter whether the orange-blue edge is on top, in the middle layer, or flipped. It is still the corner’s partner. If it is stuck incorrectly, eject it first, then bring both pieces to U and pair them.

One real-cube rep

Do this for 5 minutes: scramble, make your cross, then pause before each F2L pair. Point to a corner and say its complete chain aloud—“corner → edge → slot”—before making any turn. If the partner is not on top, name it anyway, eject it, and resume.

The win today: you stop scanning for “some edge that looks useful.” Every white corner gives you an exact two-colour search target. Ask your teacher a follow-up if a pair repeatedly feels hard to locate.
