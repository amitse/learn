Rubik’s Cube · F2L evidence practice

Write one action-ready repaired verdict

Turn two comparable Fresh E/F logs into one sentence that tells you exactly what to practise next—without inventing a cause or a second task.

⏱ 6–8 minutes · One skill: repair the earliest unsupported clause, then write one ACT verdict

The three-part sentence

Use this only after the condition fields match: same target pair, empty workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint.

Because Fresh E and F [recorded shared first mismatch], [one evidence-matched action].
1 · Locate the earliest unsupported clause
If condition drift exists, stop: collect matching evidence. Otherwise, make the evidence clause match the logs. Only then check the action.
2 · Keep the evidence literal
Name what both attempts show—not why you think it happened.
3 · Choose one matched action
Repeated target → minimally refine or slow that drill. Shared later mismatch → begin one narrow successor drill. Both clear it → retain.

An ACT verdict is a decision, not a diagnosis. No added cause, algorithm, or bonus action.

Quiz 1: choose the repair

Fresh E and F have all four matching condition fields. Both first fail destination positions. Which is action-ready?

Quiz 2: repair the earliest failure

Fresh E used the cue before setup; Fresh F used it after setup. A draft accurately says, “Collect a matching Fresh F attempt with the cue before setup.” What is the correct result?

Your 90-second cube-side practice

  1. Take two matching Fresh E/F logs from your current F2L drill.
  2. Circle their shared first mismatch. Cross out any guessed cause in your old verdict.
  3. Write exactly one Because Fresh E and F… sentence using the template. Run the gate: matching condition → literal evidence → one action.

Ask your teacher to audit your sentence if you are tempted to add “because I…” or “also…”. Those are common signs that the verdict has outrun its evidence.
