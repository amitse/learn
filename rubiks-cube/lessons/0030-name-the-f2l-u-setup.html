Name the F2L U setup before you turn

Today’s win: when Lesson 29 says a U setup is viable, say the exact move—0, U, U2, or U′—instead of cycling to discover it.

One idea: every viable row has one destination move

Keep the selected working side fixed. Your destination is always a known picture: the target corner at UFR or UFL, and its edge at UF. A U turn moves both target pieces around the top together, so each viable starting row maps to that destination by exactly one U amount.

Read the table as a plan, not a sequence. Find the starting row → say its one move → turn once → run the green-light check. Do not execute all four U amounts.

The 20-second loop

Choose the side. White Right selects front-right; white Left selects front-left. White-Up needs normalization, not a U setup.
Match one row. Locate both the target corner and matching edge in that side’s map. No row means no U setup—re-read or normalize.
Name and make one move. Say “U2” (or 0 / U / U′) before moving. For 0, make no U turn at all.
Green-light, then trigger. At the destination, verify the corner’s white sticker still faces the selected side and the edge at UF has its front sticker matching the front centre. Only then use the short trigger.

One deliberate rep

With an unsolved, ready pair on U, select the side and name its corner + edge positions. Use the map to say one setup out loud before you turn. After the move, stop for the two-part green light—do not let a correct position substitute for an orientation check.

Ask your teacher a follow-up if a map row feels unclear. Include pair colours, white direction, selected side, corner position, edge position, named setup, and the edge-centre result.
