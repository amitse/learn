Rubik’s Cube · Lesson 0024 · under 10 minutes

Read the corner before you try to pair it

In F2L, a corner can be on the U layer yet still not fit either safe pairing picture. Your win today: identify its white-sticker orientation, and use an empty working slot to normalize a white-up corner instead of guessing.

One idea: location is not orientation

With white down, look at the target corner on U. Its two non-white colours still identify the matching edge and centre-defined slot. But its white sticker tells you whether the corner is ready for one of your two known pictures.

White faces RightAt UFR, this can be the known right picture: edge at UF, front sticker matches front centre → R U R′.
White faces LeftAt UFL, this can be the mirror picture: edge at UF, front sticker matches front centre → L′ U′ L.
White faces UpNot either safe picture. A U turn changes its position, not which way white faces. Normalize first; do not force an insertion.
Recognition cue: name pair → empty slot → white direction before a trigger. White-up is a diagnosis, not a failure.

The white-up recovery loop

This is deliberately not a new case algorithm. It returns an awkward corner to the visual language you already know.

Protect an empty working slot. Put the target’s empty slot at front-right or front-left using a whole-cube y rotation. Never choose a solved pair as the workspace.
Confirm the corner is white-up. Check the target corner’s non-white colours and its matching edge. If white is on U, neither prepared insertion picture is present yet.
Use one short trigger through the empty slot. Front-right: R U R′. Front-left: L′ U′ L. This changes the corner-edge relationship; it is a controlled normalization move, not an instruction to repeat.
Stop and re-read. Locate the same corner by its colours, then use U/U′ only to build a confirmed right- or left-facing picture. Insert only after the white direction and edge-centre check agree.

A trigger can change orientation; U turns are for top-layer setup. That distinction is why inspection after the trigger matters.

Mini decision rule

White facing a side? Rotate to put the appropriate empty slot at the front, then see whether U/U′ can make one known picture.

White facing up? Do not hunt for a magic U turn. Use one protected empty-slot trigger, pause, and read the corner again.

Any doubt? Preserve solved pairs. Re-identify by the two non-white colours before every next move.

One deliberate rep

With white down, find a target F2L corner on U whose white sticker faces up (or set one up in practice). Say its pair colours and target slot. Put that empty slot at the front, execute exactly one matching short trigger, then stop. Find the corner again and use U moves only after you can name a safe right- or left-facing destination picture.

Ask your teacher a follow-up if this feels unpredictable. Report the corner’s three colours, the white-sticker direction before and after the trigger, the empty working side, and which solved pairs were protected.
