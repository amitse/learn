Plan the U setup before turning

After you normalize and re-read an F2L pair, do not spin U hoping. Today’s win: choose one setup candidate, make it once, then verify whether it created a known safe picture.

One idea: U turns move positions, not orientations

With white down, a U, U′, or U2 turn carries every top-layer piece to a new position. It does not turn a corner’s white sticker from Up to Right/Left, and it does not flip an edge. So U setup is a way to test which of the four top-layer alignments fits your already-read piece orientations.

Useful question“Which U alignment might put this already-ready pair above my empty working slot?”
Not useful“Which random U turn will make the white-up corner become right-facing?” It cannot.

The four-state setup scan

Keep an empty working slot at front-right (or front-left for the mirror). Before a trigger, inspect the four possible U-layer alignments in a fixed order:

CandidateWhat to check after it
0 (no U turn)Is the known safe picture already present?
URe-check the same pair, the empty slot, and centre match.
U2Same check; it is a distinct alignment, not “two guesses.”
U′Same check; this completes all four U positions.
Important: this is a scan, not a four-turn sequence. Choose one candidate, turn once, inspect. If it fails, undo it before trying another candidate, or stop and select a different unsolved pair.

The pre-turn script

Protect. Name the target pair and use a y rotation to place its empty slot at the working side.
Read. State the corner’s white direction and the matching edge’s front sticker versus the front centre. A white-up corner or unready edge needs normalization first, not U scanning.
Name one candidate. Say “try U′” (or 0 / U / U2) before touching the cube. You are testing an alignment, not improvising.
Verify green. After that one move, confirm a known picture: right side = white right at UFR + edge at UF whose front sticker matches the front centre; mirror left side = white left at UFL + the same edge-centre check.
Commit or reset. Green right → R U R′. Green left → L′ U′ L. Otherwise undo the candidate and re-read; do not trigger through a protected pair.

Why this helps a complete solve

Your mission is a reliable solve, not a lucky sequence. Naming the candidate separates planning from execution: a failed check costs one reversible U turn, while a blind trigger can cost a solved F2L pair.

One deliberate rep

On a cube or virtual simulator, find a target pair after a re-read. Keep its slot empty at the front. Before each U move, say the candidate aloud: “0,” “U,” “U2,” or “U-prime.” After exactly one candidate, check the corner direction and edge-centre match. Undo it if it is not green.

Ask your teacher a follow-up if a setup feels unclear. Report the pair colours, corner white direction, the candidate you tried, and whether the edge’s front sticker matched the front centre.
