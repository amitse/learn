Rubik’s Cube · under 10 minutes

Write the next F2L micro-drill

Turn a completed refined-drill verdict into one testable next practice plan—without losing a cue that already works.

The one thing to learn

After a completed four-log cycle, your next drill must follow its verdict. A successor micro-drill retains a proven cue and changes the target only when both fresh logs reveal the same later first mismatch.

Writing rule: copy the condition; name one target stage; name one checkpoint; state five repetitions; then name the two matching fresh logs that will judge it. Do not rewrite the retained cue.

One verdict → one precise plan

Suppose blue-red at an empty front-right slot has two baseline logs failing the edge-centre check. The retained refinement is the cue “front sticker, front centre”. Fresh C and D both pass that check but first fail immediately after the authorized R U R′ trigger.

Keep
Condition
blue-red; empty front-right; green-light check; retained centre cue.
Change
Target
authorized-trigger execution/hold only; checkpoint: pair is inserted immediately after R U R′.

The new drill is not “practice F2L.” It is: five blue-red attempts under that condition; say the retained centre cue; after the green light, execute one authorized trigger; inspect the slot immediately. Then collect Fresh E and Fresh F under the exact same condition.

Use this five-line template

  1. Condition: target pair + empty working-slot type + observation checkpoint.
  2. Retain: the cue or refinement supported by both fresh logs.
  3. Target: the one shared later first mismatch—not the old one.
  4. Boundary: five controlled repetitions; stop at the stated checkpoint.
  5. Test: two fresh, comparable logs. Retain the new drill only if both remove or move past this new mismatch.

If the prior verdict was “refine once,” keep the original stage as the target and change just one feature. If conditions drifted, write no new drill: repair the evidence first.

Your 4-minute action

  1. Take your latest cycle verdict and underline the old earliest mismatch.
  2. Write the retained cue exactly as used—if the verdict showed a shared later bottleneck.
  3. Name the later stage and a visible checkpoint in one short phrase.
  4. Write five controlled repetitions that stop at that checkpoint.
  5. Pre-write Fresh E and Fresh F with the same pair, workspace type, and checkpoint. Run them only after the five repetitions.
