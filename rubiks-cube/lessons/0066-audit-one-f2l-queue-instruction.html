Rubik's Cube · Lesson 66 · under 10 minutes

Audit one F2L queue instruction

A queue line is useful only if it faithfully carries one card row into cube practice. Today, catch the first thing that makes a proposed instruction unsafe.

The audit rule

Read the selected row first, then check classification → scope → one action. Stop at the first failure. An ACT line may run only that row’s approved drill. A FRESH EVIDENCE line may collect only a matching replacement attempt.

Three checks, in order

1
Classification.
ACT means perform the row’s one approved practice action. FRESH EVIDENCE means collect evidence—not a drill change.
2
Scope.
Does the instruction retain this row’s target pair, empty workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint? Do not borrow any detail from a neighbouring row.
3
One action.
Look for one main verb and one allowed action. “Then…” usually signals an imported or second action.

Worked audit

Selected Row B · FRESH EVIDENCE
Target: blue-red pair. Workspace: front-right empty slot. Cue: say “white right; edge matches front centre” before the trigger. Checkpoint: immediately before the trigger. Required next action: one matching replacement Fresh F attempt.
Proposed queue line
“Run five slow green-light reads for the blue-red pair, then collect a front-right replacement Fresh F attempt.”

Fail at check 1: classification. Row B is FRESH EVIDENCE; “run five reads” is a drill action, not evidence collection. Stop there—there is no need to praise or inspect the later clause.

Repair: “Collect one replacement Fresh F attempt for the blue-red pair with a front-right empty workspace; say the retained cue before the trigger and record the checkpoint immediately before it.”

Quick check

Row D · ACT: its comparable Fresh E/F logs both repeated a planning mismatch. Its one approved action is: “run five slow named-U-setup reads with the same green-orange pair and front-left empty workspace; checkpoint at destination positions.” Which proposed line passes?

Do it now

Choose one written queue instruction from your card. Put your finger on its source row and say:

label → four scope fields → one permitted action

If you find a mismatch, rewrite only the first failed part. If it passes, do the one instruction—then record what happened. Ask your teacher to review any line that feels ambiguous before turning.
