Rubik’s Cube · under 10 minutes

Compare a refined F2L drill with four logs

A refinement is credible only when its before-and-after logs describe the same problem under the same conditions.

The one thing to learn

After a three-log assessment says “same earliest mismatch,” you may refine the drill. To tell whether that refinement helped, compare two baseline logs with two fresh logs. Keep the target pair, empty working-slot type, and observation boundary unchanged.

Four-log rule: retain a refined drill only when both fresh comparable logs move the first mismatch later or remove it. If conditions drift, the comparison is inconclusive—not a win or a failure.

Read the evidence

Baseline A and B used the blue-red pair, an empty front-right slot, and stopped at the green-light check before any trigger. Both first failed because the edge at UF did not match the front centre. The refinement: five slower reads that point to the front sticker and front centre before naming the verdict.

Do not compare unlike attempts

Keep fixed
Target corner-edge pair, empty workspace type, and checkpoint (for example, before the trigger).
May change
One stated refinement: slower verbal read, a clearer cue, or a smaller movement boundary.
Inconclusive
Fresh logs use a different pair, a protected slot, or an after-trigger checkpoint when baselines stopped before the trigger.

Changing the practice aid is the experiment. Changing the cube condition at the same time hides what caused the result.

Your four-log loop

  1. Keep the two original baseline logs; note their shared earliest mismatch.
  2. Write one refinement that still targets that exact stage.
  3. Run five bounded repetitions.
  4. Make Fresh C and D under the original comparable condition.
  5. Verdict: both later/none → retain; either same → refine or slow; changed condition → collect comparable fresh logs.

This keeps recovery aligned with the mission: you learn where a read failed and make one controlled change, rather than restarting a whole solve.

Today’s tangible win

Take one micro-drill that repeated its earliest mismatch. Write one refinement, then make four one-line logs: A, B, C, and D. Before declaring improvement, check that C and D match the baseline condition.
