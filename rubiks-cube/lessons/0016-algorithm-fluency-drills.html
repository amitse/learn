Algorithm Fluency Drills

Build muscle memory for the 5 algorithms you'll use every solve

Why This Matters

You can now complete a solve — but if you have to think about each algorithm, you'll be slow and error-prone under pressure. Fluency means the algorithm runs on autopilot: your fingers execute it while your eyes look ahead to the next step.

Today's lesson targets the 5 algorithms that appear in almost every solve: Sune, Anti-Sune, Y-Perm, Ua, Ub. Use the drill timer and quiz below to build real reps.

🎯 Goal: Execute any of these 5 algorithms correctly, from memory, in under 5 seconds. Even at slow speed, accuracy first — speed follows.

The 5 Core Algorithms

Sune OLL / Orient corners
R U R' U R U2 R'
When to use: OLL — when you have one correctly-oriented corner (yellow facing up, top-left).
🤌 Right index push R, right thumb U, index pull R'. Rhythm: push-up-pull · push-up-up-pull
Anti-Sune OLL / Orient corners
R U2 R' U' R U' R'
When to use: OLL — mirror of Sune. When the correctly-oriented corner is top-right and the other pattern calls for it.
🤌 Same finger assignment as Sune — learn both back-to-back so your hands feel the contrast.
Y-Perm PLL / Permute corners
F R U' R' U' R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R F'
When to use: PLL — swaps two diagonal corners. If two corners look diagonal after OLL, this is your algorithm.
🤌 First half ends with F' — pause mentally there. Second half is a standard right trigger into F R F'.
Ua-Perm PLL / Cycle 3 edges
R U' R U R U R U' R' U' R2
When to use: PLL — cycles 3 last-layer edges counterclockwise (viewed from top).
🤌 The rhythm is very regular — up-down, up, up, up-down-down. Practice slow until the pattern is natural.
Ub-Perm PLL / Cycle 3 edges
R2 U R U R' U' R' U' R' U R'
When to use: PLL — cycles 3 last-layer edges clockwise (viewed from top). Mirror of Ua.
🤌 Starts with R2 which is easy — then it's the reverse rhythm of Ua. Learn Ua first, then Ub is the "undo" feel.

Algorithms sourced from ruwix.com and speedsolving.com wiki.
