Rubik’s Cube · F2L evidence practice

Repair a failed Fresh E/F verdict

Turn a misleading conclusion into one sentence that says only what two fresh, comparable attempts actually justify.

⏱ 7–9 minutes · You need: a cube only if you want to run the next drill

The one skill

A verdict audit can fail in one of three places: the attempts were not comparable, the sentence misstates the first mismatches, or its action does not follow from them. Repair the failed clause—do not invent a fuller story.

1. Check condition
2. State the observed evidence
3. Choose one matched action

Template: “Because Fresh E and F [accurate shared result], [one evidence-matched action].”

Action map

  • Both move past the target → retain the drill/cue.
  • Either repeats the target → minimally refine or slow the drill.
  • Both share the same genuinely later mismatch → begin a narrow later-stage drill.
  • Pair, workspace, retained cue, or checkpoint differs → collect matching fresh evidence.

The workflow order matters: planning → green-light recognition → authorized-trigger execution. A different label is not automatically a later mismatch.

Worked repair

Fixed condition: blue-red pair, empty front-right slot, retained verbal cue, checkpoint immediately after the authorized trigger.

Fresh E: reaches the destination picture; edge fails the centre check before any trigger.
Fresh F: reaches the destination picture; edge fails the centre check before any trigger.

Failed verdict: “Because the trigger was executed poorly, switch to the left trigger and speed up.”

The logs show no authorized trigger, so “executed poorly” is unsupported. The two actions are also unjustified.

Repaired verdict: “Because Fresh E and F both repeat the edge-readiness failure before the trigger, slow or minimally refine the green-light drill.”

Notice what stayed fixed: the target, condition, and scope. The repair is deliberately small.

Quick check

Scenario: Fresh E and F use the same pair, workspace, cue, and checkpoint. Both get past the old green-light target, then both first fail immediately after an authorized trigger.

Which repair is defensible?

One-minute practice: repair this

Condition: Fresh E used an empty front-right slot; Fresh F used an empty front-left slot. The verdict says: “Because E and F both cleared the target, retain the drill.”

Say or write the one clause that must change before the next practice run.

Model repair: “Because Fresh E and F used different empty-workspace types, collect matching fresh attempts before making a verdict.” The evidence is condition drift, so neither “cleared the target” nor “retain” is established.

Take it to the cube

  1. Choose one existing F2L micro-drill and preserve its pair, workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint.
  2. Record Fresh E and Fresh F with their first mismatch.
  3. Write one verdict. Audit it by underlining the condition, evidence, and single action. If any part is unsupported, replace only that part.
