Classify repaired Fresh E/F verdicts

After repairing a conclusion, decide whether it is ready to guide the next drill—or whether the evidence still needs repair.

⏱ 7–9 minutes · One concept: action-ready vs evidence-needed

The one skill

A minimally repaired verdict is action-ready only when its two Fresh attempts are comparable and the replacement action matches their first mismatches. If the repair identifies condition drift, it is evidence-needed: collect matching fresh attempts before changing practice.

1. Read condition
2. Read first mismatch
3. Classify the verdict

Comparable evidence + one matched action = act now. Condition drift = collect evidence first.

Two repaired verdicts

Verdict A

Fixed condition: same blue-red pair, empty front-right slot, retained cue, and pre-trigger green-light checkpoint.

E/F: both pass the old green-light target; both first fail after an authorized trigger.

“Because Fresh E and F share a later post-trigger mismatch, retain the cue and begin a narrow execution-stage drill.”

Class: action-ready. The attempts are comparable; the shared mismatch is genuinely downstream.

Verdict B

Condition: E uses an empty front-right slot; F uses an empty front-left slot. The cue and pair are otherwise the same.

E/F: both appear to clear the old target.

“Because the workspace type drifted, collect matching fresh attempts before making a practice decision.”

Class: evidence-needed. This is the correct repair, but it authorizes no drill change yet.

Decision rule

  • Act now: same pair, workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint; both observations support exactly one next action.
  • Collect evidence: a condition differs, or the repaired sentence can only truthfully say that comparability was lost.
  • Narrow the repair: if the condition matches but the sentence still adds a cause, second action, or an inaccurate first mismatch.

Do not treat a correctly written “collect evidence” instruction as a failed lesson. It protects the controlled condition that makes your next cube attempt interpretable.

Quick check

Fresh E and F use the same pair, empty workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint. E repeats the old edge-readiness failure; F reaches the trigger but then has a later failure. The repaired verdict says: “Because either fresh attempt repeats the old target, slow the green-light drill.”

How should you classify it?

One-minute classification

Case: Fresh E uses the retained verbal cue; Fresh F silently drops it. Their proposed sentence says, “Because both cleared the target, move to an execution drill.”

Which minimal repair and classification fit?

Field rule: record the classification beside each repaired verdict: ACT only when condition, evidence, and one action align; otherwise write FRESH EVIDENCE and repeat the same condition. This prevents an attractive success story from quietly changing your drill.

Take it to the cube

  1. Run—or retrieve—two Fresh E/F attempts for one micro-drill.
  2. Underline pair, workspace, cue, checkpoint, first mismatch, and action in the verdict.
  3. Write ACT if all four condition fields match and the action fits; otherwise write FRESH EVIDENCE and collect the matching pair next.
