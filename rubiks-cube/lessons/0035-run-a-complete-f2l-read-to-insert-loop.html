Rubik’s Cube · F2L · under 10 minutes

Run a complete F2L read-to-insert loop

You can now recover without guessing. This lesson connects the parts into one bounded loop: read one pair, choose its side, name one setup, earn the green light, then insert exactly once.

The loop has one gate

The trigger is last. R U R′ or L′ U′ L is an insertion only after the complete green light. A setup turn or normalization never carries permission forward.
Name the pair and home slot. Read the corner’s two non-white colours, find the matching edge, and use the fixed centres to identify its empty slot.
Read the corner; choose the side. White facing Right selects an empty front-right slot; white facing Left selects front-left. White-Up has no side: it needs one controlled normalization and a fresh read.
Name one U setup—or 0. With a viable position template, predict exactly 0, U, U2, or U′ to reach right UFR + UF or left UFL + UF.
Check the full green light. At the destination, white faces the selected side and the matching edge at UF has its front sticker matching the front centre.
Insert once—or stop. Green light: use the matching short trigger. No green light: do not append a trigger; choose the least-disruptive next action from a fresh read.

One concrete run

Starting observation

The white-blue-red corner and blue-red edge identify the blue-red slot. The slot is empty. The corner’s white sticker faces Right, so place that slot at front-right with a whole-cube y rotation if needed.

Plan, then verify

Your position template says one U carries the pair to UFR + UF. Say “U,” make it once, then stop. At the new picture, white still faces Right and the edge’s front sticker matches the front centre.

Authorized finish

Both checks pass, so execute R U R′ once. The pair is inserted. If either check had failed, the correct finish would have been no trigger: re-read and select a ready pair, a named positional route, or one normalization.

Fast decision map

Green-lit now?Insert once.
Viable positions?Name one U setup, then re-check.
Neither?Normalize once through an empty slot, then fresh-read.

A different pair that is already green-lit beats more manipulation of this one. Protecting solved pairs matters more than solving pairs in a fixed order.

Authorization drill

Choose the one next action. Feedback is immediate.

1. A white-green-orange corner faces Left. Its matching edge and its centre-defined slot are identified; after a named setup they are at UFL + UF. White still faces Left, and the edge’s front sticker matches the front centre. What now?

2. A right-side named setup reaches UFR + UF, but the corner is white-Up. No other pair is ready. What is the next action?

3. After your fresh read, the original pair is unready, but a different pair has both green-light checks. What is least disruptive?

Five-minute physical drill

  1. Scramble or pause during F2L with at least one empty slot. Pick one target pair.
  2. Say aloud: “pair, slot, side, setup, green light.”
  3. Make only the named setup (or deliberately choose 0), then inspect both green-light checks.
  4. If both pass, insert once. If one fails, say “not authorized” and stop—choose the least-disruptive recovery instead of forcing the trigger.
  5. Repeat twice. Your win condition is three deliberate decisions, not three fast inserts.
