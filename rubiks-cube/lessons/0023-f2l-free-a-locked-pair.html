Free a locked F2L piece before you pair

Sometimes your identified corner and edge are not usefully available: one is trapped in a slot or their current relationship cannot reach either safe picture with U turns. The win is to free one piece to U, then return to the known setup → trigger loop.

One idea: normalize, don’t force

A U turn repositions top-layer pieces, but it cannot eject a piece that is trapped in a bottom/middle slot. With white down, make that piece’s slot your empty working slot at the front. Use the matching short trigger to lift it to U:

Use a trigger only through an empty slot. It is an ejection tool here—not a random repair move. A solved pair is protected; rotate the whole cube first if necessary.

The controlled recovery loop

Name the pair. Corner’s two non-white colours → matching edge → centre-defined target slot.
Choose the workspace. Put the target’s empty slot at front-right or front-left with a whole-cube y rotation.
Free the trapped piece. At front-right use R U R′; at front-left use L′ U′ L. Inspect: the piece should now be available on U.
Re-read, then set up. Use U/U′ to make the familiar right or left picture from Lessons 20–22. Only then run its insertion trigger.

The same trigger has two jobs depending on the starting state: in a prepared picture it inserts; at an empty slot it can free a misplaced piece. The visual check after the move tells you which job you just did.

Safe picture reminder

Right: empty front-right; corner at UFR, white facing right; matching edge at UF, front sticker matches front centre → R U R′.

Left: empty front-left; corner at UFL, white facing left; matching edge at UF, front sticker matches front centre → L′ U′ L.

After freeing a piece, stop. Do not guess a second trigger. Re-identify the corner, edge, and empty slot; then make a confirmed picture.

One deliberate rep

Find an F2L corner or edge trapped in a wrong slot (or create one while practising). Identify its partner and target. Bring the empty target slot to the front, free the trapped piece with the appropriate short trigger, then pause and rebuild one safe picture before inserting. Confirm every previously solved pair survived.

Ask your teacher a follow-up if this fails. Report: the piece colours, its slot, the empty working slot, the trigger used, and where the piece appeared after it.
