Collect and classify a matching Fresh E/F pair

Two fresh attempts are useful only if they preserve the audited condition. Your skill today: decide whether they are ready for a verdict—or whether one must be replaced.

⏱ 6–8 minutes · One skill: classify a Fresh E/F evidence pair

What makes the pair verdict-ready?

After an audited plan, run Fresh E and Fresh F as separate attempts. For each, record the first mismatch at the pre-stated checkpoint. Then compare the same four condition fields you planned.

Same four fields + one recorded first mismatch each = verdict-ready. Any new drift = replace that attempt; do not interpret the pair yet.

The collection loop

  1. Copy the passed plan. Do not improve it between E and F.
  2. Run Fresh E. At its checkpoint, write the first mismatch—or “none at this checkpoint.”
  3. Run Fresh F under the copied condition. Write its first mismatch at that same checkpoint.
  4. Classify before concluding. If all four fields match, label the pair verdict-ready. If one changed, label that attempt replacement needed and collect a new one.

The classification comes before a retain/refine verdict. A valid miss is still evidence; an unlike attempt is not.

Quick check: evidence or replacement?

Fresh E uses the audited front-left workspace and records at destination positions. Fresh F uses the same pair, cue, and checkpoint—but an empty front-right workspace. What is the correct classification?

Quick check: classify, don’t explain

Both attempts preserve all four fields. Fresh E’s first mismatch is “white-up corner at the pre-trigger check”; Fresh F has the same first mismatch. What can you say now?

Pocket rule: Match first, interpret second. Check pair → workspace → cue timing → checkpoint. Four matches means verdict-ready; one drift means replace the drifted attempt. A miss is data, not disqualification.

Take it to the cube

  1. Use yesterday’s audited Fresh E/F plan.
  2. Make Fresh E and Fresh F on separate lines; record only the first mismatch at the stated checkpoint.
  3. Compare all four fields after both attempts.
  4. Write exactly one label: verdict-ready or replacement needed: [field].

Ask your teacher to inspect the two lines with you if a condition or checkpoint is ambiguous.
