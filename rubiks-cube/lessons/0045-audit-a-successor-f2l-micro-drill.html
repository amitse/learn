Audit a successor F2L micro-drill

Before practising your next drill, verify that it tests the new bottleneck—not a different condition or a discarded solution.

The one thing to learn

A successor micro-drill audit is a pass/fail check on a written plan. It passes only when it keeps the proven cue and comparable condition, targets one shared later mismatch, uses that stage’s visible checkpoint, and pre-writes comparable Fresh E and Fresh F logs.

Audit rule: if any one of those five parts is missing, do not run the drill yet. Repair only the missing part; do not invent a broader practice plan.

The five checks

1 · retain
Proven cue stays.
If “front sticker, front centre” cleared the old edge-readiness mismatch in both fresh logs, it belongs in the new condition.
2 · compare
Condition stays.
Keep the same target pair and empty-workspace type. Otherwise Fresh E/F cannot test the new drill.
3 · isolate
One later target.
It must be the first mismatch shared by both fresh logs—not merely something that happened once.
4 · observe
Checkpoint fits.
For execution, inspect immediately after the authorized trigger. For recognition, stop before the trigger.
5 · test
E and F match.
State two fresh logs with the same pair, workspace, retained cue, and checkpoint after five repetitions.

Audit one plan

Evidence: Baselines A/B first failed the edge-centre check for the blue-red pair at an empty front-right slot. With the cue “front sticker, front centre”, Fresh C/D both pass that check and first fail immediately after an authorized R U R′.

Written plan: “For five blue-red/front-right attempts, say the centre cue; verify the green light; execute R U R′; inspect the slot immediately. Then log Fresh E and Fresh F with the same pair, workspace, cue, and checkpoint.”

This passes: the cue and condition remain; execution is the shared later target; the checkpoint is immediately after the trigger; and E/F are comparable.

Notice what is not being tested: whole-solve speed, a different pair, or green-light recognition. Those would add variables.

Your 3-minute action

  1. Write your intended successor plan in one sentence.
  2. Underline the retained cue and circle the target pair + empty workspace.
  3. Draw a box around exactly one checkpoint.
  4. Write Fresh E and Fresh F before turning the cube.
  5. If you cannot point to all five audit checks, repair the sentence first.
