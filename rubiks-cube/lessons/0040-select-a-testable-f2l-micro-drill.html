Rubik’s Cube · under 10 minutes

Select a testable F2L micro-drill

Choose the practice plan that can actually tell you whether your current F2L bottleneck moved.

The one thing to learn

After an F2L attempt, two drill plans can sound equally productive. Pick the one that tests one earliest mismatch under a comparable condition, at an observable boundary. That lets you improve reliably rather than merely practise more.

Selection rule: the better drill names the same pair/workspace, limits five repetitions to the earliest failing stage, and says exactly what to observe. “Do F2L” is not a test.

Compare the plans

Evidence: two comparable logs first fail at the edge-centre check: the matching edge reaches UF, but its front sticker does not match the front centre. The corner/edge destination positions are reached.

Plan A · broad

“Do five complete F2L pairs. Try to make the edge work. Then time a solve.”

It mixes setup, green-light reading, triggers, and timing. A later result cannot show whether the edge-centre read improved.

Plan B · testable

“With the same blue-red pair and empty front-right slot, run five planned setups. At UF, stop before a trigger and say whether the front edge sticker matches the front centre. Then make one fresh comparable log.”

It holds the condition and isolates the exact checkpoint where the first mismatch occurs.

Use this quick filter

1 · Same condition?
Does it retain the target pair and type of empty working slot from the evidence logs?
2 · One stage?
Does it practise planning or green-light recognition or authorized-trigger execution—not all three?
3 · Visible stop?
Can you point to the checkpoint: destination positions, the two-part green light, or immediately after an authorized trigger?
4 · Honest verdict?
After five reps, does one fresh comparable log decide: first mismatch later/absent → retain; same earliest mismatch → refine or slow?

A plan missing any filter is an intention. Add the missing line; do not widen the target.

One repair, not a rewrite

Suppose a plan says: “Practise corner orientation five times.” Its target is plausible, but its checkpoint is missing. Repair only that gap:

Repair: “With the same pair and empty front-right slot, make five planned setups; at UFR + UF, stop before a trigger and name white Right, Left, or Up. Then run one fresh comparable log.”

Now a White-Up first mismatch can be observed before execution muddies the evidence. This supports the mission’s recovery goal: a wrong read becomes a small, reversible diagnosis rather than a reason to restart.
