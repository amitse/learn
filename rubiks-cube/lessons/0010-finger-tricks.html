Rubik's Cube · Lesson 10

Finger Tricks

You know every algorithm. This lesson removes the pauses between moves — so each algorithm executes as one fluid motion, not a sequence of individually-placed turns.

Why Bother?

After your first few full solves, the bottleneck isn't the algorithms — it's the pauses. You finish R, stop, readjust your grip, then do U. Each pause costs 1–2 seconds. A solve with 50+ moves can lose 30 seconds to regripping alone.

Finger tricks assign specific fingers to specific moves. Your hand stays on the cube. Each move flows directly into the next without letting go.

This is not speed-cubing. The goal matches your mission: a reliable, repeatable solve that doesn't stall mid-algorithm.

When to use this: Once you've done at least one unaided full solve. If you're still shaky on any algorithm, review its lesson first — finger tricks only help when the sequence itself is solid.

The Standard Grip

Almost every beginner algorithm uses only three faces: R, U, and F. Your hands should always be positioned to reach all three without moving your whole hand away from the cube.

Sequence 1 — R U R′

This 3-move fragment is the most common in the entire beginner method. It appears in white corners, second layer, yellow cross, Sune, and the corner permutation algorithm. Master this before anything else.

The goal: R flows directly into U (middle finger takes over), and U flows directly into R′ (thumb comes forward). Three moves, one gesture.

Sequence 2 — R U R′ U′ (Right Trigger)

The full Right Trigger. The first three moves are what you just practised. The U′ is the tricky fourth move — your left thumb sweeps in from the left side of the top face.

After R′, your thumb is near the front-right corner of the cube. Your left thumb simultaneously arrives on the top face. The handoff should be seamless — not a pause-and-reposition.

Sequence 3 — F R U R′ U′ F′ (Yellow Cross)

The F-wrap is the hardest part. F uses your right thumb pushing down; F′ uses your right index lifting up. The 4 middle moves are your Right Trigger — which you just practised.

Practice Drill — 20 Right Triggers

Execute R U R′ U′ twenty times in a row, without stopping. Use the timer. A comfortable pace is 12–15 seconds for 20 reps (roughly 0.6–0.75s per trigger). Don't rush — smooth and uninterrupted beats fast.

After 10–15 reps you should feel the sequence become automatic. That's muscle memory forming. The fingers know where to go before your brain explicitly tells them.

What's Next

You now have every algorithm and the physical technique to execute them smoothly. The natural next step is look-ahead — planning the next stage while you're still executing the current one, so there's no pause between stages. That's the first step toward CFOP-style efficiency.

Before that: do 5 full solves applying finger tricks throughout. After each solve, note which stage still causes a hesitation — that's the one to drill in isolation.
