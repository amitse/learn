Classify an audited mixed triage card

Keep every row’s evidence local, then choose the one queue action that is actually ready.

⏱ 7–9 minutes · One skill: turn an audited card into one safe next action

Audit first. Classify second.

Lesson 63 repaired the earliest problem in every card line. Now read the repaired lines as a small queue. A line is not “better” because its prose sounds confident; it is ready only when its own condition, evidence, and one instruction all agree.

1
Mark each line. ACT means its two Fresh attempts are comparable, the literal mismatch is accurate, and it has one matched instruction. FRESH EVIDENCE means a condition drift still requires a replacement attempt.
2
Keep rows separate. An ACT on row A never repairs row B’s cue, workspace, pair, or checkpoint.
3
Choose one queue action. If any row is FRESH EVIDENCE, collect its named replacement before making a practice change from that row. Otherwise, run one ACT instruction—do not combine two drills.

A classification is a boundary: evidence-needed rows ask for matching observation; action-ready rows authorize one narrow practice action.

Read this audited card

A

ACT Both logs share pair, front-right empty workspace, cue before setup, and pre-trigger checkpoint. Both first miss: edge-centre check. Instruction: run five slow edge-centre recognition repetitions with that cue.

B

FRESH EVIDENCE Fresh F used an empty front-left workspace rather than the front-right workspace. Instruction: collect a replacement Fresh F with empty front-right workspace.

C

ACT Both logs share all four fields. Both first miss: post-trigger placement. Instruction: run five authorized-trigger execution repetitions.

A and C can guide their own drills. B cannot: its evidence boundary is still open. Do not use A’s or C’s result to decide what B means.

Queue choice for B: collect replacement Fresh F with the front-right workspace — then classify B from matching evidence.

The one-action rule

When several lines are ACT, pick one narrow instruction for the next cube-side block. When a selected line says FRESH EVIDENCE, the replacement attempt itself is the whole next action—not a replacement plus a new drill.

Not allowed: “A is ACT, so I will also change B’s trigger.”
Allowed: “B is FRESH EVIDENCE, so I will collect B’s matching replacement attempt.”

This protects the mission: reliable solving comes from seeing what one controlled attempt says, rather than piling changes onto a confusing cube state.

Quiz 2: choose the queue action

An audited card has row A marked ACT. Row B has cue-timing drift and says FRESH EVIDENCE → collect Fresh F with cue before setup. What is the correct next action for row B?

2-minute cube-side task

  1. Take two recent Fresh E/F verdicts or use the A/B examples above.
  2. For each, say: ACT or FRESH EVIDENCE, and name the condition/evidence that proves it.
  3. Choose one line only. If it is ACT, do its one drill; if it is FRESH EVIDENCE, collect its one matching replacement attempt.
  4. After the attempt, write one literal first-mismatch observation—no explanation yet.

Ask your teacher to inspect your card if you are tempted to use one row’s result as evidence for another.
