Rubik’s Cube · under 10 minutes

Write one minimal F2L drill refinement

When a four-log comparison is inconclusive, repair the experiment—not the whole solve.

The one thing to learn

An inconclusive comparison has not shown whether your refinement helped. First identify why: condition drift means collect comparable fresh logs; a repeated earliest mismatch means change exactly one drill feature. Do not change both.

Minimal-refinement rule: keep the target pair, empty workspace type, and checkpoint fixed. Change one cue or one pace only, then specify two fresh comparable logs.

D drifted in pair and workspace. This result is inconclusive; it does not prove the cue works or fails.

Choose the smallest justified next plan

If drift caused it
Do not add a new cue. Re-run Fresh D as blue-red / empty front-right / before-trigger checkpoint. The original refinement remains the only experimental change.
If mismatch repeated
Keep the same condition and checkpoint. Change one feature, such as slowing the spoken cue to two beats. Do not also add a new trigger drill.
Write two logs
Fresh C: condition → first mismatch
Fresh D: same condition → first mismatch

The goal is a verdict you can trust. A smaller plan is stronger because it leaves fewer possible causes.

Your 4-minute action

  1. Copy the condition from Baseline A: pair, empty workspace, and checkpoint.
  2. Circle the cause: condition drift or same earliest mismatch.
  3. Write one sentence: “I will change only ___.” If it was drift, write “I will collect a matching fresh log”; that is not a new refinement.
  4. Make two fresh logs under the copied condition.
  5. Compare both with the baselines: later/none on both → retain; same mismatch → make one smaller refinement; drift → collect matching evidence.

That controlled loop protects your already solved pairs and helps you recover reliably instead of guessing through F2L.
