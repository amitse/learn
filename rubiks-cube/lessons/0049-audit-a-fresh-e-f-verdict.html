Audit a Fresh E/F verdict

Check whether a written F2L practice verdict says exactly what two fresh observations justify—and nothing extra.

The one thing to learn

A verdict can sound sensible yet still be unusable. Before acting on it, run a verdict audit: compare its evidence claim and its one proposed action against Fresh E and Fresh F.

Pass rule: a verdict passes only when the fresh attempts are comparable, the sentence states their actual first-mismatch evidence, and its single action matches that evidence. Anything added beyond that is an unsupported change.

The 3-question audit

  1. Comparable? Do E and F use the same target pair, empty-workspace type, retained cue, and checkpoint?
  2. Accurate? Does the sentence name the actual first mismatch in each fresh log?
  3. Matched? Does its action follow the rule: both clear → retain; either repeats target → refine/slow; both share a later mismatch → successor; drift → repair evidence?

Stop at the first “no.” Do not repair the cube, add an algorithm, or explain a cause inside the verdict.

Common audit failures

Evidence drift
“E and F improved” when their pair or workspace differed. Correct action: collect matching fresh attempts.
Action mismatch
“Refine planning” even though both fresh logs cleared planning. Correct action: retain it; use the later shared mismatch.
Unsupported addition
“Change the algorithm because execution failed.” Correct action: omit the cause/change; first test execution narrowly.
Two actions
“Slow the drill and start a new pair.” Correct action: choose only the one action supported by E/F.

Today’s tangible win

Take your next one-sentence Fresh E/F verdict and mark it Pass only after answering: comparable, accurate, matched. If it fails, rewrite only the unsupported part—then ask your teacher to check the two logs and your revised sentence.
