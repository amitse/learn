Write an evidence-matched Fresh E/F verdict

A verified matching pair is not the decision itself. Turn its shared first mismatch into exactly one narrow next action—without adding a cause, a new algorithm, or a second plan.

⏱ 6–8 minutes · One skill: write one verdict from matching fresh evidence

The verdict is one sentence

First confirm the pair is verdict-ready: Fresh E and Fresh F used the same target pair, empty workspace type, retained cue (including timing), and checkpoint. Then name what both attempts actually show.

Because Fresh E and F [shared first-mismatch evidence], [one evidence-matched action].

Write the observation first. Then choose one action. Do not explain why the miss happened.

Both clear the targetRetain the drill.
Either repeats the targetMinimally refine or slow the drill.
Both share a later mismatchBegin one narrow later-stage drill.
Any condition driftsCollect replacement evidence—not a drill verdict.

Worked verdict

Both fresh attempts preserve the blue-red pair, an empty front-right workspace, the cue before setup, and the pre-trigger green-light checkpoint.

Fresh E
first mismatch: edge-centre check fails at the pre-trigger green light

Fresh F
first mismatch: edge-centre check fails at the pre-trigger green light

Verdict: Because Fresh E and F both repeat an edge-centre failure at the pre-trigger green light, slow or minimally refine the green-light drill.

This is narrow: it reports the shared evidence and selects one action. “The edge algorithm is wrong” is not in the logs, so it does not belong in the verdict.

Build it in two passes

  1. Copy only shared facts. State the common first mismatch (or that both moved past the target).
  2. Match the action to that fact. Clear → retain; repeated target → slow/refine; shared later mismatch → successor; drift → replacement evidence.
  3. Delete extras. Remove causal stories, new algorithms, new target pairs, and any second action.

Quick check: choose the verdict

Fresh E and F match all four condition fields. Both move past the old edge-readiness target; both first fail after the authorized trigger. Which verdict fits?

Quick check: no invented explanation

Fresh E and F match. E repeats the target green-light failure; F does too. Which clause must be removed from this draft? “Because both attempts repeat the green-light failure, slow the recognition drill because the right trigger is definitely executed incorrectly.”

Pocket rule: shared evidence → one matched action. A verdict is not a diagnosis. If the evidence says “repeat,” refine or slow; if it says “later together,” narrow the next drill; if conditions drift, replace evidence first.

Take it to the cube

  1. Use a verdict-ready Fresh E/F pair from your collection sheet.
  2. Write one sentence with “Because Fresh E and F…”
  3. Underline the shared first mismatch and circle one action.
  4. Before practising, cross out every causal explanation or extra action.

Ask your teacher to inspect the sentence if the two first mismatches seem similar but occur at different checkpoints.
