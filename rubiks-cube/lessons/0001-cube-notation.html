Cube Notation

The alphabet of every algorithm. You can't read a solution until you know this.

Win for this lesson: By the end you can read any standard Rubik's Cube move notation — U, R, F, L, D, B and their primes — without looking it up.

Move Notation

A letter alone means 90° clockwise (when looking directly at that face). A letter followed by ʼ (prime) means 90° counter-clockwise. A letter followed by 2 means 180° — direction doesn't matter.

Notation Meaning How to remember
U Turn the top layer clockwise (looking down) Screw lid tightening
Uʼ Turn the top layer counter-clockwise Screw lid loosening
U2 Turn top layer 180° Either direction, ends the same
R Turn the right layer clockwise (looking from right) Right hand pulls away from you at the top
Rʼ Turn the right layer counter-clockwise Right hand pulls toward you at the top
F Turn the front layer clockwise (looking at you) Right side goes down
Fʼ Turn the front layer counter-clockwise Left side goes down
L Turn the left layer clockwise (looking from left) Left hand pulls away from you at the top
D Turn the bottom layer clockwise (looking up from below) Opposite of U
B Turn the back layer clockwise (looking from behind) Least used — appears mainly in OLL/PLL later

Source: Ruwix — Standard Cube Notation

Reading an Algorithm

Algorithms are just sequences of these moves. Example — the Sexy Move, the most common pattern in beginner solving:

R Uʼ Rʼ U

Say it out loud as you read: "Right, Up-prime, Right-prime, Up." That's it. Every algorithm in the beginner method is just 4–8 moves like this.
