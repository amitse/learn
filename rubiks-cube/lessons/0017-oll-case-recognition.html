Rubik’s Cube · Lesson 0017 · under 10 minutes

Read the yellow top before you turn

Make one decision tree automatic: identify your 2-Look OLL case from the yellow stickers, then choose the right setup and algorithm. This removes trial-and-error from a complete solve.

The one skill

OLL means orienting the last layer: every top sticker becomes yellow. In 2-Look OLL, always read edges first, then corners. Do not use a U turn to “set up” a case after the cross—use a whole-cube y rotation so the yellow cross stays intact.

Decision rule: no yellow cross → solve edges. yellow cross → solve corners. A y rotation changes which face is front; a U turn changes the last-layer pieces’ positions.

Look 1: read only the four edge positions

Dot

Apply the edge algorithm; then read the new L or line.

L shape

Hold the yellow L at back + left, then apply it.

Line

Hold the line horizontal (left ↔ right), then apply it.

Cross

Edges are done. Ignore side colours and inspect the corners.

Edge algorithm: F R U R′ U′ F′. The dot needs up to two applications total; an oriented L or line needs one. Ruwix: OLL overview

Look 2: read the corner stickers

All four corner stickers yellow-up? OLL is finished. Continue to PLL.
Exactly one corner yellow-up? Rotate the whole cube until it is back-left for Sune, or back-right for Anti-Sune.
Zero corners yellow-up? Do Sune from any angle once. It creates a one-corner case; then read it again.

Sune: R U R′ U R U2 R′   Anti-Sune: R′ U′ R U′ R′ U2 R

The visual “one-corner” rule is the useful recognition shortcut. Whole-cube rotations are safe setup; U turns are not. See the Speedsolving Wiki OLL reference for the broader OLL family.

One real-cube rep

On your next scramble, pause after F2L. Point to the yellow edge pattern, say “dot/L/line/cross,” then execute. Once the cross exists, count yellow corner stickers and announce “zero / BL Sune / BR Anti / done.” Only then touch the cube.

If a result surprises you, do not restart. Re-read the top from the current state: cross first, corners second. Ask your teacher a follow-up question if one visual case remains confusing.
