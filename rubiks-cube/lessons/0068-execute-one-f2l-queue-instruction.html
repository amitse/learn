Rubik's Cube · Lesson 68 · under 10 minutes

Execute one F2L queue instruction

A repaired instruction becomes useful only when one cube attempt follows it exactly and produces its stated observation.

Today's one skill

Run one row-local action, then stop at its checkpoint. Do not improve the plan during the attempt. The observation is the output; a new verdict comes later.

The execution loop

1
Read the selected row aloud. Name its classification, target pair, empty-workspace type, retained cue, checkpoint, and one permitted action.
2
Set the condition. Put that pair and workspace on the cube. Use the retained cue at the specified time—do not substitute a different pair or checkpoint.
3
Do exactly one action. An ACT row means its approved drill. A FRESH EVIDENCE row means one matching replacement attempt.
4
Stop and log the checkpoint literally. Record what was visible there, not a cause, a verdict, or a follow-up action.

Worked example: execution is not a verdict

Selected Row B · ACT
Target: blue-red pair. Workspace: front-right empty slot. Retained cue: say “white right; edge matches front centre” before the trigger. Checkpoint: immediately before the trigger. One action: run five slow named-U-setup reads.

For one repetition, keep precisely that condition, say the cue before the trigger, and inspect at the stated checkpoint.

Valid checkpoint log: “Rep 1 — before trigger: corner at UFR, white faces Right; edge at UF, front sticker matches front centre.”

This is an observation, not “the drill worked,” “I rushed,” or “now collect Fresh E.” Those are different claims/actions and are outside this one queue execution.

Quick check

Selected Row D · FRESH EVIDENCE: green-orange pair; front-left empty slot; say “white left” before the trigger; checkpoint immediately before the trigger; one action: collect one matching replacement Fresh E attempt.

Which log correctly completes that instruction?

Do it now

Choose one repaired queue instruction. Copy it onto a note, then perform only its permitted action once. At the exact checkpoint, write one literal line:

attempt label → checkpoint → visible state

Stop there. Preserve the line for the next lesson, where it can be interpreted without guessing. Ask your teacher if the source row or checkpoint is unclear before turning.
