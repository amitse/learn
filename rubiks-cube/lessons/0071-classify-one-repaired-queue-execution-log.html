Rubik's Cube · Lesson 71 · under 10 minutes

Classify one repaired queue-execution log

Before a repaired F2L log can influence practice, decide whether it is a valid checkpoint record or still needs replacement evidence.

Today’s one skill

Run the scope gate again after a repair. A polished sentence is valid only when it preserves the source instruction’s four fields and stops at a literal observation.

The source instruction is the test

ACT · target: blue-red pair · workspace: front-right empty slot · cue: say “white right; edge matches front centre” before trigger · checkpoint: immediately before trigger · action: one slow named-U setup read.

Classify a proposed repaired log against this instruction—not against whether it sounds plausible. The four fields are target pair, workspace type, cue/timing, and checkpoint.

The two possible results

VALID CHECKPOINT LOG
All four fields match the instruction, and the line ends with an observation that can be seen at the checkpoint.
REPLACEMENT EVIDENCE NEEDED
A field actually drifted during the cube attempt. A text edit cannot make a late cue, different workspace, or post-trigger observation into matching history.

The 3-check gate

1
Scope: compare the four fields one by one. Stop at the first mismatch.
2
Literal observation: does the line describe only what was visible at the stated checkpoint?
3
Boundary: no cause, verdict, or next action may trail the observation. If a repair merely deleted one, that is fine—provided the actual attempt met the scope.

Worked classification

The writer corrected an added conclusion. The actual attempt used the source pair, slot, cue before the trigger, and checkpoint:

Rep 4 — immediately before trigger: front-right slot empty; blue-red corner has white facing Right; matching edge’s front sticker matches the front centre.

VALID CHECKPOINT LOG Every scope field matches and the statement is observable. It does not say that the insertion will work, why a prior attempt failed, or what to do next.

Contrast: if the real cue was spoken after the setup, deleting that phrase would hide a physical drift. The honest classification is replacement evidence needed.

Quick check

The source instruction above is unchanged. The actual cube attempt used the blue-red pair and front-right empty slot, but the cue was spoken after the named-U setup. The proposed repair says:

Rep 5 — immediately before trigger: front-right slot empty; corner white faces Right.

How should you classify it?

Do it now

Take one repair from Lesson 70. Copy its source instruction, then write one classification line:

[VALID CHECKPOINT LOG / REPLACEMENT EVIDENCE NEEDED] — [first matching fact or first drifted field].

If it is valid, leave the log as evidence only. If it needs replacement, copy all four source fields into the next attempt; do not change the F2L drill yet. Ask your teacher if the wording and the physical attempt seem to tell different stories.
