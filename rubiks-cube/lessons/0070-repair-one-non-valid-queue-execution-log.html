Repair one non-valid queue-execution log

Make a rejected F2L checkpoint line usable without rewriting the attempt or inventing a better result.

Today's one skill

Repair only the first failed part of the written log. Preserve every matching scope field and every literal observation. Do not re-run the cube, diagnose it, or add a new action.

Source instruction: your authority

ACT · blue-red pair · front-right empty slot · say “white right; edge matches front centre” before trigger · checkpoint: immediately before trigger · action: one slow named-U setup read.

A valid checkpoint log must preserve the four scope fields: target pair, workspace, cue/timing, checkpoint; then it must be a literal observation only.

The repair order

1
Find the label from Lesson 69. Is it drifted execution or an unbounded conclusion? Identify the first fault, not every imperfection.
2
Replace only that fault. For drift, copy the source instruction’s first changed scope field. For an unbounded line, delete the first added cause, verdict, or follow-up action.
3
Stop at a literal checkpoint observation. Keep the actual visible state. Repairing a log is not permission to claim success or alter what happened.

One repair, not a rewrite

This log has a checkpoint drift:

DRIFTED EXECUTION
Rep 2 — after the trigger: front-right slot empty; target corner white faces Right.

The first failure is after the trigger. The instruction requires immediately before trigger. Keep the matching pair, workspace, and literal observation; replace only the checkpoint phrase:

REPAIRED CHECKPOINT LOG
Rep 2 — immediately before trigger: front-right slot empty; target corner white faces Right.

This repairs the written record’s scope. It does not pretend the original physical observation happened before the trigger. If the time of the observation truly changed, the honest remedy is a new matching attempt—not relabelling history.

Important boundary: edit words vs. replace evidence

Use a text repair only when the line was written wrongly but the real attempt met the source condition. If the cube attempt itself drifted—wrong checkpoint, pair, workspace, or cue—keep it classified as drifted and collect a new matching attempt later. Never repair evidence by changing its claimed timing.

Quick check

The source instruction above is unchanged. The real attempt was at the correct pre-trigger checkpoint, but the writer appended a conclusion:

Rep 3 — immediately before trigger: front-right slot empty; corner white faces Right; therefore the setup is fixed and I will do five more.

Which is the minimal honest repair?

Do it now

Take one non-valid line from your Lesson 69 classification. Write two lines:

  1. First failure: [scope field / added conclusion or action]
  2. Repaired log: [one literal checkpoint observation]

If the physical attempt—not merely the wording—changed a scope field, write replacement attempt needed instead. Ask your teacher if you cannot separate a writing error from changed evidence.
