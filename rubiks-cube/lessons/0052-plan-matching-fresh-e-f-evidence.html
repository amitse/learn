Rubik’s Cube · F2L evidence practice

Plan matching Fresh E/F evidence

When a verdict says FRESH EVIDENCE, make the next two attempts genuinely comparable—so they can support a decision.

⏱ 6–8 minutes · One concept: preserve the four condition fields

The one skill

Condition drift does not mean the drill failed. It means the old comparison cannot tell you what caused the result. Your repair is a collection plan: write the conditions that Fresh E and Fresh F must share before touching the cube.

Copy the condition, not the outcome. Two matching attempts create evidence; two different attempts create a story.

1 · Target pairThe same corner-edge pair, identified by its two side colours.
2 · Workspace typeThe same empty working-slot type: for example, front-right.
3 · Retained cueThe same proven verbal or visual cue used in the earlier drill.
4 · CheckpointThe same visible boundary where you record the first mismatch.

Build one collection plan

Your last verdict was evidence-needed: Fresh E used an empty front-right slot, Fresh F used an empty front-left slot. The target was the blue-red pair; the retained cue was “name white direction, then match edge to centre”; the checkpoint was the pre-trigger green light.

Write before turning:
Fresh E and Fresh F: blue-red pair · empty front-right slot · say “white direction, edge-to-centre” · record first mismatch at pre-trigger green light.

This plan does not promise that E and F will succeed. A repeated failure is still useful evidence, because the comparison is controlled.

Run it in four short moves

  1. Freeze the four fields. Copy pair, workspace type, cue, and checkpoint from the evidence-needed verdict.
  2. Prepare Fresh E. Use the stated workspace and cue; stop at the checkpoint and log the first mismatch.
  3. Prepare Fresh F the same way. Do not “improve” the cue, switch sides, or move the checkpoint between attempts.
  4. Only then compare. If a field drifted again, replace the drifted attempt; if all four match, write the Fresh E/F verdict.

Quick check

Baseline condition: orange-blue pair, empty front-left workspace, cue “say the named U setup,” checkpoint = destination positions. Which plan restores comparability?

One-minute field decision

You collect Fresh E with the blue-red pair, front-right workspace, retained cue, and pre-trigger checkpoint. Fresh F uses all of those—but you forget the cue until after you make the U move. What is the next action?

Pocket rule: before each Fresh attempt, say: pair, slot, cue, checkpoint. If any one differs, label that attempt invalid for the comparison and collect a matching replacement—not a new hypothesis.

Take it to the cube

  1. Find your most recent verdict marked FRESH EVIDENCE.
  2. Write its four fields in one line using the plan format above.
  3. Run Fresh E and Fresh F, logging only the first mismatch at the shared checkpoint.

Ask your teacher to check your one-line collection plan before you run it.
