Rubik's Cube — Glossary

All terms introduced across lessons, in order of appearance.

Pieces

Centre
The fixed middle piece of each face. There are 6 — one per face. They never move relative to each other. The colour of a centre defines that face's colour.
Edge
A piece with 2 coloured stickers, located between two centres. There are 12 edges. Edges form the cross and second-layer slots.
Corner
A piece with 3 coloured stickers, located at the intersection of 3 faces. There are 8 corners.

Faces & Moves

U
Up face (white). U = clockwise 90° turn; U' = counter-clockwise; U2 = 180°.
D
Down face (yellow).
F
Front face (green by default). F = clockwise when looking at it.
B
Back face (blue).
R
Right face (red). R = clockwise from the right side.
L
Left face (orange). L = clockwise from the left side.
' (prime)
Counter-clockwise turn of that face. E.g. R' = Right face counter-clockwise.
2
180° turn (double). F2 = Front face rotated 180°.

Solve Stages

White Cross
Step 1. Place the 4 white edge pieces on the white face (D, held down), each matching the adjacent side centre. Built via the Daisy Method.
Daisy
Intermediate state: 4 white edges on the U face (yellow up) with white stickers facing up, surrounding the yellow centre — like a flower. Used as a building step for the white cross.
Layer 1
The white face + the ring of 4 white corners. Complete after the White Cross (lesson 2) and White Corners (lesson 3).
F2L
"First Two Layers" — a method (and stage name) that combines the white cross, white corners, and second-layer edges into one unified process. Corner-edge pairs are found and inserted together, replacing stages 1–3 of the beginner method. Uses the Right Trigger and Left Trigger; no new algorithms needed.
OLL
"Orientation of the Last Layer" — orienting all top-layer stickers yellow-up. Taught in lessons 5–6.
PLL
"Permutation of the Last Layer" — moving pieces into their final correct positions. Taught in lessons 7–8.

Key Algorithms

F2
Lock a daisy petal into the white cross: align petal above matching centre with U turns, then F2.
F U R U'
Lift a white edge from the bottom layer (white sticker sideways) into the daisy.
R U R′ U′
Right Trigger — the single most important beginner algorithm. Inserts a white corner when white sticker faces right. Also used for middle-layer edges (Lesson 4) and beyond.
F′ U′ F
Insert a white corner when white sticker faces the front face.
R U R′ U′ ×3
Insert a white corner when white sticker faces up. Executed as a single continuous 12-move sequence (3 Right Triggers with no pause).
R U R′
Extract a white corner from the bottom layer (3-move shorthand) when it's in the wrong slot. Lifts it to the top layer for repositioning.

Solve Process Concepts

White Corners
Step 2 of Layer 1 (Lesson 3). Insert all 4 white corner pieces into the bottom layer after the white cross is complete. Hold white DOWN throughout.
Layer 1
Complete when both the white cross and all 4 white corners are solved. The entire white face + surrounding side ring.
Extraction
Moving a piece that's in the wrong bottom-layer slot up to the top layer so it can be repositioned. Done with R U R′ for corners.

Second Layer (Layer 2) Algorithms

Right Insert
U R U′ R′ U′ F′ U F — inserts an edge into the front-right middle slot when the edge's front-facing sticker matches the front centre. Case A.
Left Insert
U′ L′ U L U F U′ F′ — inserts an edge into the front-right middle slot when the edge's right-facing sticker matches the right centre. Case B. Mirror of the Right Insert.
y rotation
A whole-cube rotation turning the cube left (counter-clockwise when viewed from above). Used to reposition the target slot to front-right without altering the cube state.
Extraction (L2)
Popping a mis-inserted middle-layer edge back to the top layer by applying an insertion algorithm from above. The wrongly placed edge is displaced to U, then reinserted correctly.
Side sticker
For an edge sitting on the U face: the sticker that faces a side (not upward). Used to determine Case A vs Case B for second-layer insertion.

Yellow Cross Cases (OLL Step 1)

Dot
0 yellow edge stickers facing up on the top face. Only the yellow centre is visible. Apply F R U R′ U′ F′ from any hold, producing an L-shape or Bar, then orient and apply once more.
L-shape
2 adjacent yellow edges up, forming an "L" pattern. Orient so yellow edges are at Back and Left (elbow pointing back-left), then apply F R U R′ U′ F′ once → Cross.
Bar
2 opposite yellow edges up, forming a straight line across the top. Orient so the bar is horizontal (left ↔ right), then apply F R U R′ U′ F′ once → Cross.
Yellow Cross
All 4 yellow edge stickers face up. OLL Step 1 is complete. Corner stickers are irrelevant at this stage.

Yellow Cross Algorithm

F R U R′ U′ F′
Yellow Cross algorithm. Orients top-layer edges to produce the Yellow Cross. The middle 4 moves are the Right Trigger (R U R′ U′) wrapped in F … F′. Apply at most twice from any starting case.

Yellow Corner Orientation (OLL Step 2)

Sune
R U R′ U R U2 R′ — orients yellow corners when the one correct corner (yellow-up) is at back-left (UBL). Named after speed-cuber Sune Berger. In 2-Look OLL, Sune solves the UBL case with one execution.
Anti-Sune
R′ U′ R U′ R′ U2 R — the exact mirror of Sune. Use when the one correct corner is at back-right (UBR). Every move is the prime/inverse of the corresponding Sune move.
2-Look OLL
An efficient subset of OLL using only 2 algorithms (Sune + Anti-Sune) plus the yellow cross algorithm. Solves OLL in at most 2 looks / 2 algorithm executions. Full OLL has 57 algorithms; 2-Look OLL reduces this to 3 algorithms total (including yellow cross).
OCLL
"Orientation of Corners, Last Layer" — the second look in 2-Look OLL. After the yellow cross exists, OCLL orients the 4 corner pieces so yellow faces up on all of them. Solved with Sune or Anti-Sune based on which corner has yellow on top and where it sits.
BR (corner)
The back-right corner of the top face. When applying Sune, a yellow-facing sticker must be at BR first. Sune leaves this corner unchanged.
UBL / UBR
Top-Back-Left and Top-Back-Right — the two setup positions for 2-Look OLL. UBL = use Sune; UBR = use Anti-Sune. Achieved by rotating the whole cube (y / y'), never the U layer.
U2
180° turn of the U (top) face. The key move in Sune (before the final R′) that distinguishes it from the Right Trigger variants.
OLL Step 2
Orienting the 4 yellow corners after the Yellow Cross is formed. Complete when all 9 top-face stickers show yellow. In 2-Look OLL, solved using Sune or Anti-Sune.
0-corner case (OLL)
A state where no yellow corner sticker faces up after the yellow cross. Apply Sune from any hold once — this always produces a 1-correct-corner case which is then solved with Sune or Anti-Sune.

Yellow Corner Permutation (PLL Step 1)

Headlights
Two corner stickers on the top strip of one face that share the same colour. Indicates those two corners are correctly paired. The setup cue for the corner permutation algorithm: rotate headlights to the back face before applying.
Top strip
The two corner-sticker squares in the top-left and top-right positions of a side face — the only stickers relevant during PLL Step 1.
Adjacent (case)
Headlights exist on exactly one face. Apply the corner permutation algorithm with headlights at the back. The most common PLL corner case.
Diagonal (case)
No headlights on any face. Apply the corner permutation algorithm from any hold once; headlights will appear, then solve the resulting adjacent case.
Corner permutation algorithm
R U R′ F′ · R U R′ U′ · R′ F · R2 U′ R′ — cycles FL, FR, and BL corners, leaving the BR corner fixed. Apply with headlights at the back.
PLL Step 1
Permuting the 4 top-layer corners after OLL is complete. Solved in at most 2 applications of the corner permutation algorithm.
Y-Perm
F R U' R' U' R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R F' — the standard 2-Look PLL algorithm for cycling 3 last-layer corners. The back-left corner is the anchor (stays put). Setup: place the matching corner pair at the back before executing.
2-Look PLL
A 2-step method to permute the last layer using only 3 algorithms (Y-Perm, Ua-Perm, Ub-Perm) in at most 4 executions. Look 1 handles corners (Y-Perm); Look 2 handles edges (Ua or Ub). Completes the CFOP beginner framework.

Last Layer Edge Permutation (PLL Step 2)

Ua perm
R U' R U R U R U' R' U' R2 — cycles three last-layer edges counter-clockwise (viewed from above): front→left, left→right (via back arc), right→front. The back edge is unchanged. Applied when the front edge belongs in the left slot.
Ub perm
R2 U R U R' U' R' U' R' U R' — the exact inverse of Ua; cycles edges clockwise: front→right, right→left (via back arc), left→front. Applied when the front edge belongs in the right slot.
3-cycle (edge)
The case where exactly 1 last-layer edge is correctly placed. The other three form a 3-piece cycle. Solved with one application of Ua or Ub (back edge = anchor).
0-correct (edge)
A last-layer state where no edge sticker matches the centre below it. Apply Ua once from any hold — this converts the state into a 3-cycle which is then solved normally. Never indicates a mis-scrambled cube.
PLL Step 2
Permuting the 4 last-layer edges after PLL Step 1 (corner permutation) is complete. The final step of the beginner layer-by-layer method. Solved in at most 2 algorithm applications using Ua or Ub.
Anchor edge
The one correctly placed last-layer edge used as a reference point in the 3-cycle case. Hold yellow up and rotate the whole cube (y / y') until the anchor edge is at the back before applying Ua or Ub.

Practice Methods

Stage drill
A practice technique where the solver scrambles the cube and repeatedly solves only one specific stage (e.g., white cross only) — typically 10+ times. Builds automatic recognition and execution for that stage.
Full slow solve
A full cube solve done deliberately slowly, pausing before each algorithm to confirm orientation and case. Builds correct process habits before speed is introduced.
Dirty dozen drill
12 consecutive full solves in one sitting, tracking the slowest or most hesitant stage after each solve, then drilling that stage in isolation for 5 minutes. Targets the weakest link systematically.
Look-ahead
Planning the next stage while still executing the current one, eliminating full inspection pauses between stages. Works by redirecting the mental bandwidth freed when an algorithm runs on autopilot. No new algorithms required — it is a purely attentional skill. The five key transition windows are: (1) last cross edge → scan top corners, (2) flip cube → scan second-layer edges, (3) last 2nd-layer insertion → count yellow cross petals, (4) after yellow cross → count oriented yellow corners, (5) last Sune → glance for headlights.
Transition window
A specific moment during a solve where look-ahead can be applied — scanning for the next stage's starting state while the hands are still finishing the current stage. Each of the seven beginner stages has a natural transition window where the relevant information is visually available.
Pause audit
A deliberate practice drill: count every full stop (where both hands and planning halt simultaneously) during a complete solve, then reduce that count by one per session. The targeted approach to building look-ahead — drill only the longest/hardest pause in isolation.
Algorithm recall
A drill where the solver writes or verbalises all algorithms from memory, without the cube, then checks accuracy against a reference. Identifies gaps in pure memorisation separate from physical execution.

Recovery & Diagnosis

Orientation error
The most common root cause of unexpected algorithm outcomes: applying a correct algorithm with the wrong face held as "front" or wrong whole-cube orientation. The algorithm itself is never wrong — only the hold can be.
Stage independence
The property of the layer-by-layer method where completing one stage does not disrupt previously completed stages, provided the correct algorithms are applied. Enables recovery without restarting from Stage 1.
y rotation
A whole-cube rotation around the vertical axis, equivalent to turning the entire cube as if rotating the U face. Used during Stages 6–7 to reposition faces without disturbing layer state. Must be used instead of U moves when repositioning during PLL.

OLL Recognition

OLL decision tree
The two-step recognition process for 2-Look OLL: first identify the yellow edge pattern (Dot, L-shape, Bar, or Cross); only after the Yellow Cross exists, identify the yellow corner state (0, 1, or 4 corners facing up).
Case caller
A deliberate-practice cue: name the visual OLL case and the intended setup/algorithm aloud before turning. Separates recognition from execution and prevents trial-and-error turns.
Safe setup (OLL)
A whole-cube y or y′ rotation used to place an L-shape, line, or correct yellow corner in the required position. Unlike a U turn, it preserves the relationship of last-layer pieces to the fixed side centres.

Finger Tricks (Physical Technique)

Finger trick
A specific finger assignment for a given face move, designed so adjacent moves in an algorithm can be executed without releasing the cube or repositioning the whole hand. Eliminates the micro-pauses that slow down solves.
Standard grip
The resting hand position for beginner algorithms: right ring+pinky on the R face, right thumb near the F face, right middle resting on the U face, left thumb ready to sweep U. Keeps all R/U/F moves within reach of the fingers already placed.
R (finger trick)
Right ring + pinky push the top edge of the right face forward (away from the solver).
R′ (finger trick)
Right thumb pulls the front edge of the right face toward the solver (downward).
U (finger trick)
Right middle finger sweeps the top face to the left.
U′ (finger trick)
Left thumb sweeps the top face to the right. This is the hand-bridging move in the Right Trigger: left thumb must arrive during the R′ motion, not after it.
F (finger trick)
Right thumb pushes the front face downward (clockwise).
F′ (finger trick)
Right index finger lifts the front face upward (counter-clockwise). Uses a different finger than F.
Right Trigger (finger)
R U R′ U′ as a single continuous gesture: ring+pinky → middle → thumb → left thumb. The R′→U′ handoff (right thumb → left thumb) is the hardest transition and must be practised until seamless.
Muscle memory
The state where a sequence is executed automatically by the fingers without conscious step-by-step instruction. Formed through repeated isolated drill of short fragments (e.g., R U R′) before combining into full algorithms.

F2L — Intuitive First Two Layers

Pair
The fundamental unit of F2L: one corner + one edge that share two non-white colours and belong in the same slot. There are 4 pairs in total, one per slot.
Slot
The corner-edge position in the solved first two layers, identified by its two adjacent side-centre colours (e.g., "Blue-Red slot"). Each slot accepts exactly one pair.
Right Trigger (F2L)
R U R' — the 3-move trigger used to insert a pair into the right-front slot when the corner's white sticker faces the Right face and the matching edge is at the front-top position.
Left Trigger (F2L)
L' U' L — mirror of the Right Trigger; inserts a pair into the left-front slot.
Ejecting
Using a trigger at a slot to lift a wrongly-placed or unoriented piece from the bottom layer up to the top layer (U face), without disturbing any other slot. The first step when a piece is stuck in the wrong position.
Case 1 (F2L)
Both corner and edge are already in the top layer. The ideal starting state: use U moves to align, then insert with a trigger.
Case 2 (F2L)
Corner is in the top layer but the matching edge is stuck in the middle layer. Fix: eject the edge to the top with a trigger, then solve as Case 1.
Case 3 (F2L)
Corner is in a bottom-layer slot but the wrong one (or unoriented). Fix: execute a trigger at that slot to eject the corner, then solve as Case 1.
Case 4 (F2L)
Both corner and edge are in wrong slots. Fix: eject one piece at a time to the top layer, then solve as Case 1 once both are on U.
Pair recognition
The visual skill of reading a white corner’s two non-white colours to identify its matching edge and its target slot. Example: a white-blue-red corner pairs with the blue-red edge and belongs between the blue and red centres.
Colour chain
A deliberate F2L recognition cue: name corner → matching edge → centre-defined slot before turning. It prevents searching for a merely convenient edge or inserting into the wrong slot.

Timed Solving & Practice

Benchmark solve
The first complete, unaided solve of a scrambled cube — regardless of time. Used as the baseline against which all future progress is measured. A 15-minute benchmark solve is as valid as a 2-minute one.
Scramble
A specific sequence of moves (typically 20–25) applied to a solved cube to randomise its state before a timed solve. Standard scrambles are computer-generated to avoid human bias. Every valid scramble is solvable.
Ao5 (Average of 5)
A standard cubing metric: the mean of 5 consecutive solves, dropping the best and worst times. The primary benchmark used beyond beginner level (replaces raw best time). cstimer.net calculates this automatically.
DNF (Did Not Finish)
A solve where the solver did not arrive at a fully solved state, or made an illegal move. In timed practice, a DNF scores infinity and is dropped when calculating Ao5.
+2
A 2-second penalty added to a timed solve for stopping the timer with exactly one face layer unresolved. Common in competition; in casual practice it signals a near-miss.
cstimer.net
The standard free online timer used by cubers. Generates WCA-legal scrambles, tracks Ao5/Ao12/session averages, and runs entirely in the browser. Recommended for all timed practice sessions.

F2L Setup Discipline

Working slot
The one unsolved F2L corner-edge slot deliberately placed at front-right (or front-left for a mirror) before preparing a pair. It is the safe workspace for the next insertion.
Setup move (F2L)
A move, usually a U turn, used to change the relationship of the target corner and edge before inserting. U turns preserve completed bottom F2L slots while repositioning top-layer pieces.
Protected pair
An F2L pair already solved in its slot. During setup, it should not be used as the slot for a trigger; use a whole-cube rotation to bring the empty target slot forward instead.

F2L Pair Formation

Pairing picture
A deliberately prepared corner-edge relationship on the U layer that lets a short trigger join the pieces. It includes both piece identity and orientation, not merely their locations.
UFR / UF
Position shorthand: UFR is the upper-front-right corner position; UF is the upper-front edge position. With an empty front-right working slot, Lesson 20’s safe case places the corner at UFR and its edge at UF.
Right-facing F2L case
A basic pairing picture: the target corner is at UFR with white facing Right, while its matching edge is at UF with its front sticker matching the front centre. Execute R U R′ to join and insert the pair into the front-right slot.
Centre check (F2L)
A setup validation: in the right-facing case, the matching edge’s front-facing colour must equal the front centre colour. Centres—not convenience—confirm the edge orientation.
UFL
Position shorthand for the upper-front-left corner. In Lesson 21’s mirror case, the target corner sits at UFL with its white sticker facing Left.
Left-facing F2L case
The mirror of the basic right-facing case: with an empty front-left slot, place the target corner at UFL with white facing Left and its matching edge at UF with the front sticker matching the front centre. Execute L′ U′ L to join and insert the pair.
Mirror trigger (F2L)
The left-side counterpart to a right-side trigger: reverse the working side and each turn direction together. For the basic pair picture, R U R′ at front-right mirrors to L′ U′ L at front-left.
Separated-pieces setup
An F2L preparation where the target corner and matching edge are both on U but not yet in a pairing picture. With an empty working slot protected, use U or U′ to turn their relationship into a known right- or left-facing picture, then use its trigger.
Destination picture (F2L)
The verified safe pairing picture that a setup move aims to create. For the basic right case: corner at UFR with white facing Right plus matching edge at UF; for the mirror: corner at UFL with white facing Left plus matching edge at UF. The edge’s front sticker must match the front centre.
Locked F2L piece
A target corner or matching edge that is trapped in a wrong bottom/middle slot, or otherwise unavailable for a safe top-layer pairing picture. A U turn alone cannot free a piece from a slot.
Ejection trigger (F2L)
A short trigger used through an empty working slot to lift a misplaced piece to U: R U R′ at front-right or L′ U′ L at front-left. After it, inspect and rebuild a known pairing picture before inserting.

F2L Orientation Control

Corner orientation (F2L)
The direction a corner’s white sticker faces while the corner is on U: Right, Left, or Up. It is separate from the corner’s location and pair identity. Right/Left can fit a known safe picture; White-Up requires controlled normalization first.
White-up corner
An F2L corner on the U layer with its white sticker facing the U face. It is not immediately in either the basic right-facing or left-facing pairing picture. A U turn changes its position but not its orientation.
Normalization (F2L)
One short trigger through an empty working slot used to change an awkward corner-edge relationship into one that can be inspected and set up. It is followed by a mandatory re-read—not repeated blindly.
Orientation scan
A pre-trigger check: identify the target pair and empty slot, then name the corner’s white-sticker direction. This distinguishes a ready Right/Left picture from a White-Up corner requiring normalization.

F2L Edge Readiness

Edge-centre check (F2L)
A pre-trigger validation for a matching edge at UF: its front-facing sticker must match the front centre. It confirms the edge’s current orientation and relationship in a known safe pairing picture.
Edge readiness
The condition in which a target F2L edge is not merely the correct colour pair, but is positioned and oriented to support a confirmed right- or left-facing insertion picture.
Two-part green light
The insertion rule: verify the corner’s white-sticker direction and the matching edge’s front-centre match before using a short F2L trigger. If either check fails, set up and re-read rather than forcing the trigger.

F2L Edge Normalization

Unready edge
A matching F2L edge whose current position/orientation does not pass the edge-centre check for a known insertion picture. It is still the correct partner; it is simply not ready for that hold.
Normalization loop
A controlled F2L recovery loop: name the pair; protect an empty working slot; make one short trigger to change the relationship; stop; then re-identify and re-read before seeking a verified pairing picture.
One-trigger rule
During normalization, apply the chosen short trigger exactly once, then inspect. Repeating it blindly turns a controlled recovery move into uncontrolled scrambling.
Read → normalize → re-read
The decision sequence for an edge that fails the centre check: diagnose the unready relationship, change it safely through an empty slot, then verify corner direction and edge-centre agreement anew before inserting.

F2L Setup Planning

Setup candidate
One deliberately selected U-layer alignment — 0 (no turn), U, U2, or U′ — tested once before an F2L insertion. It is named before turning and always followed by a full green-light check.
Four-state U scan
The finite set of top-layer alignments available while holding the same working slot: 0, U, U2, and U′. It is a diagnostic scan, not a four-turn sequence: test one state, inspect, and undo or re-read if it fails.
Reversible setup
A setup move whose outcome is inspected before a trigger is used. When a U candidate does not create a verified pairing picture, undoing it preserves completed F2L pairs and keeps the cube state understandable.

F2L Working-Side Selection

Side cue (F2L)
The ready target corner’s white-sticker direction on U, used to select the insertion workspace: white facing Right means front-right; white facing Left means front-left. White-Up is not a side cue and requires normalization first.
Working-side selection
The deliberate decision to place the centre-defined empty target slot at front-right or front-left before planning U setup. It comes after pair recognition and corner-orientation reading, and before a U candidate is tried.
Viewpoint vs. alignment
The division of jobs between a whole-cube y rotation (choose the working viewpoint without changing the cube state) and a U move (test a top-layer alignment within that viewpoint).

F2L Setup Pre-check

Position template (F2L)
A corner-and-edge position relationship on U that a common U rotation can carry to a known F2L pairing picture. It filters whether any U setup candidate is viable before the solver turns.
Position filter (F2L)
The pre-turn test that compares a selected-side target corner and matching edge with a position template. A failure means no 0, U, U2, or U′ setup can create the basic picture; re-read or normalize instead.
Positional viability
The limited claim made by a matching position template: one U setup can place the pieces in the required locations. It does not establish orientation or authorize insertion; the white-direction and edge-centre checks still apply.

F2L Named Setup

Named U setup
The one planned U-layer move—0, U, U2, or U′—that carries a viable corner-edge position template to its selected-side F2L destination picture. It is said before turning, not discovered by cycling U moves.
0 setup
A deliberate choice to make no U-layer turn because the target pair already occupies the destination positions: UFR + UF on the right side or UFL + UF on the left side. It still requires the full green-light check.
Destination map (F2L)
A lookup from a viable starting corner-edge row to its exact named U setup. It converts the Lesson 29 positional filter into one predicted action while leaving orientation validation separate.

F2L Green-Light Diagnosis

Positional success
The limited result of a named U setup that places a target pair at the destination locations: UFR + UF on the right or UFL + UF on the left. It does not by itself authorize an insertion trigger.
Insertion authorization
The full F2L green light: destination positions plus the correct corner white-sticker direction and a matching edge at UF whose front sticker agrees with the front centre.
Green-light failure
A state where a planned setup has reached useful locations but either the corner-orientation or edge-centre check fails. The safe action is to stop, protect the empty working slot, then re-read or normalize once—not to force an insertion trigger.
Corner-orientation failure
A green-light failure in which the destination corner has white facing Up rather than the selected Right or Left side. U turns reposition this corner but do not change that orientation.

F2L Recovery Choice

Least-disruptive recovery
A priority rule after an F2L green-light failure: insert a different fully ready pair first; otherwise make one controlled normalization through an empty working slot; then re-read. It avoids disturbing protected pairs or guessing with repeated turns.
Position failure (F2L)
A state where the target pair is not yet at the selected destination positions. If its position template is viable, use its one named 0, U, U2, or U′ setup, then perform the full green-light check.
Edge-readiness failure
A green-light failure where the matching edge reaches UF but its front sticker does not agree with the front centre. Do not insert; normalize once through an empty slot and re-read.
Recovery priority
The order for a failed F2L check: (1) insert any different fully green-lit pair, (2) use a known positional setup where applicable, (3) normalize exactly once through an empty slot, then inspect. Never chain recovery triggers blindly.

F2L Controlled Recovery

Controlled recovery cycle
A bounded F2L sequence: read the failure; choose one safe action through an empty working slot; execute it once; then re-read pair identity, matching edge, centre-defined slot, and green light. It ends at the re-read, not at a guessed insertion.
Recovery boundary
The rule that one normalization trigger is the entire action until fresh observations are made. A second trigger without re-reading is guessing, not recovery.
Fresh read
The mandatory post-normalization inspection. Because the trigger changes the piece relationship, all previous claims about position, corner orientation, and edge readiness must be checked again.

F2L Fresh-Read Decisions

Fresh-read fork
The post-normalization decision rule: re-identify the pair, matching edge, centre-defined slot, and empty workspace; then choose exactly one next action—insert a fully green-lit pair, make a named U setup for a viable positional template, or normalize once and re-read.
Positional route
A verified position template showing that one exact 0, U, U2, or U′ setup can carry a selected pair to its right-side (UFR + UF) or left-side (UFL + UF) destination positions. It is not insertion authorization.
Action label (F2L)
The deliberately named next action after a fresh read: insert, named U setup, or normalize. Selecting one prevents accidental trigger chaining and requires a new green-light check after any state-changing move.

F2L Read-to-Insert Loop

Read-to-insert loop
A bounded F2L workflow: identify the corner-edge pair and centre-defined empty slot; select the working side from the corner’s white direction; name one viable 0, U, U2, or U′ setup; verify the full green light; then insert once only if authorized.
Trigger gate
The final authorization boundary before an F2L insertion trigger. A right trigger requires the corner at UFR with white facing Right and a matching edge at UF whose front sticker matches the front centre; the left trigger mirrors this at UFL with white facing Left. Position alone never opens the gate.
Deliberate 0 setup
A named choice to make no U-layer turn because the pair already occupies the destination positions. It is still followed by both green-light checks before a trigger is used.
Not authorized (F2L)
The correct conclusion when either green-light check fails after a planned setup. Stop instead of appending a trigger; insert a different ready pair if available, or normalize once through an empty working slot and take a fresh read.

F2L Attempt Audit

Observation log (F2L)
A one-line record of a single F2L attempt: the named plan, first observed mismatch, diagnostic label, and one change for the next attempt. It turns a physical attempt into evidence rather than a vague impression.
Planning error (F2L)
The first mismatch occurs before the expected destination positions: the pair, centre-defined slot, working side, or named U setup was misread or predicted incorrectly.
Green-light failure
Destination positions were reached, but either the corner white-direction or the edge-centre check fails. It blocks insertion; re-read or normalize once through an empty working slot rather than forcing a trigger.
Execution error (F2L)
Both green-light checks passed before an authorized trigger, but the expected insertion did not result. Audit the hold or move execution only after re-establishing an understandable state.
First mismatch
The earliest point at which an F2L attempt differs from its named plan. It is the diagnostic target because later errors can be consequences of it.

F2L Micro-Drills

Micro-drill (F2L)
A short, deliberately bounded practice set focused on one evidence-backed bottleneck rather than a whole solve. Lesson 37 uses five controlled repetitions of planning, green-light recognition, or trigger execution.
Repeated earliest mismatch
The same first diagnostic label across two or more F2L observation logs. It selects the next micro-drill: before destination positions = planning; after positions but before a trigger = green-light recognition; after a verified green light and trigger = execution or cube hold.
Evidence set (F2L)
Two or more comparable observation logs used to choose or assess a focused drill. If labels differ, target the earlier workflow failure because later observations depend on it.

F2L Micro-Drill Assessment

Three-log assessment
An evidence check using two comparable baseline logs, five focused repetitions, and one fresh comparable attempt. It tests a selected micro-drill without confusing repetition with improvement.
Comparable attempt
An F2L attempt observed with the same target pair, empty working-slot type, and checkpoints as the baseline. Comparable conditions make a change in the first mismatch meaningful.
Later mismatch
A first mismatch that occurs at a later workflow stage than the repeated baseline mismatch. It is evidence that the earlier bottleneck improved and that the next safe practice target is now visible.
Retention rule (F2L)
After five controlled repetitions, retain a micro-drill as improved only if the third attempt’s first mismatch disappears or moves later. The same earliest mismatch means improvement is not yet demonstrated; refine or slow the drill.

F2L Micro-Drill Specification

Micro-drill specification
A written, bounded plan made from a three-log assessment: comparable condition, five-repetition scope, observation checkpoint, and retain/change decision rule. It is written before turning.
Practice condition (F2L)
The fixed context for a comparable drill: the same target corner-edge pair, the same type of empty working slot, and the same diagnostic checkpoints. It makes the later fresh log interpretable.
Observation checkpoint
The exact visible boundary at which a drill is assessed. For planning, it is destination positions; for green-light recognition, it is the white-direction and edge-centre check before a trigger; for execution, it is the result immediately after an authorized trigger.
Drill boundary
The rule limiting five repetitions to one workflow stage. It prevents a broad full solve or unauthorized trigger from substituting for evidence about the current earliest mismatch.
Refinement rule (F2L)
If the fresh comparable log repeats the same earliest mismatch after five repetitions, do not claim improvement. Change one part of the drill or slow its read, while retaining the same evidence-backed bottleneck.

F2L Testable Drill Selection

Testable micro-drill
A focused F2L practice plan whose result can be interpreted: it keeps a comparable pair/workspace condition, isolates one earliest workflow stage, names an observable checkpoint, and ends with a fresh comparable log.
Observable checkpoint
The specific visible boundary used to assess one drill: destination positions for planning, the two-part green light before a trigger for recognition, or the immediate post-trigger result for execution.
Drill repair (F2L)
Adding the one missing testable element—usually the comparable condition or observable checkpoint—to a vague practice plan without changing its evidence-backed bottleneck.
Comparable condition
The retained context that makes two F2L logs meaningfully comparable: the same target corner-edge pair, type of empty working slot, and diagnostic boundary.

F2L Refined-Drill Comparison

Four-log comparison
An assessment of one refined F2L drill using two baseline logs and two fresh logs. The comparison is interpretable only when the pair, workspace type, and observation checkpoint match across the sets.
Refinement (F2L)
One explicit change to a drill that still targets the same earliest mismatch—for example, a slower verbal cue or a clearer visual check. It is the experimental change, not a reason to alter the cube condition too.
Condition drift
A change between baseline and fresh F2L logs in the target pair, empty working-slot type, or observation boundary. It makes the result inconclusive because the refinement is no longer the only meaningful change.
Four-log retention rule
Retain a refined drill only when both fresh comparable logs show that the baseline earliest mismatch is absent or has moved later. If either repeats it, refine or slow the drill; if conditions drift, collect fresh comparable logs.

F2L Minimal Refinement

Minimal refinement (F2L)
One deliberate change to a drill after a repeated earliest mismatch under comparable conditions, while target pair, empty workspace type, and observation checkpoint remain fixed. A slower cue or clearer verbal check qualifies; adding another drill does not.
Evidence repair (F2L)
Replacing a fresh log that drifted from the baseline condition with a matching fresh log. It restores an interpretable comparison; it is not itself a refinement.
Attribution boundary
The rule that one refinement is the only intentional change between comparable baseline and fresh logs. It lets the solver attribute a later or absent mismatch to the tested change rather than to altered conditions.
