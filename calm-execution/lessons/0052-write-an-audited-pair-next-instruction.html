Calm Execution · Lesson 52

Write an audited-pair next instruction

Convert a classified pair into one sentence for two ordinary attempts—small enough to enact, specific enough to audit.

One skill: write the supported next move

Use this after Lesson 51’s classification, and keep the cue and exact two-minute action visible in the sentence. The classification decides the move; it does not invite a redesign.

For the next two ordinary attempts: [retain and gather records / one already-specified matching test]. After [stable cue], I will [exact two-minute action]. Then I will audit whether I followed this instruction.

If the pair was mixed, working, or unclear, write retain and gather two readable records. Only cue twice supports one already-specified cue test; only start-side twice supports one already-specified setup or opening-action test. This is a practical experiment rule, not evidence that two records establish a cause.

The stable-context idea behind retaining a cue is consistent with Gardner, Lally & Wardle’s review of cue-driven habit formation; this lesson applies it as a modest design choice, not a guarantee. Source: Making health habitual (2012).

10-minute applied exercise

  1. Look at the two labels from your classified, audited pair.
  2. Select the supported move below. Do not invent a fresh test; use one only if it was already specified.
  3. Write your stable cue and the physical two-minute action exactly as you will do them.
  4. Copy the resulting sentence beside the next two records. At the next review, audit enactment before reading outcomes.

Instruction builder

The aim is one calm intentional start, not catch-up work or a longer session. Stopping after the two-minute action remains valid. Ask your teacher if a test is not already specified or a record is hard to classify.
