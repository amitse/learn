Calm Execution · Lesson 62

Audit a classified-pair instruction

Before you read outcomes, check whether your exact two-attempt instruction was actually used.

8–12 minutes

One skill

Compare the sentence you wrote in Lesson 61 with each of its two ordinary attempts. Give each attempt one label: followed, not followed, or unclear.

Audit gate: if either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue or start outcomes yet. Only two followed labels create a pair ready for cue-then-start classification.

Repeated behaviour in a consistent context can become more cue-driven over time, but this small audit does not establish cause. It is a practical way to keep one planned change readable. See Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Real-world exercise

  1. Open your Lesson 61 sentence and the two attempts it covered. Do not reconstruct missing details from memory.
  2. For each attempt, ask only: did I use the stated cue, two-minute action, and any named test? If the record cannot show this, choose unclear.
  3. Use the audit below. Copy its result into your normal log. If either label is not followed, repeat the same sentence for two ordinary attempts—no catch-up work and no new change.

Two-attempt audit

Finish calmly

A label is evidence, not a grade. A repeated instruction is not a punishment; it is a way to make the next pair readable. Ask your teacher: “Do these labels match the evidence, or should I mark an attempt unclear?”
