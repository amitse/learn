Calm Execution · Lesson 76 · 10 minutes

Write the next audited instruction

Turn the pair you classified into one small instruction you can actually audit—rather than a promise to “do better.”

Name the same observable cue, the same exact two-minute action, and one next move for two ordinary attempts.

The one skill

  1. Use only the result of a pair that passed the enactment audit and was classified cue-first.
  2. If the pair was mixed, working, or unclear: write retain and gather—keep the cue and action for two ordinary attempts.
  3. If the same readable break appeared twice: write only the one already-specified matching test. Keep all other parts stable.
  4. Do not add a second test, catch-up work, or a verdict about yourself.

This is a practical experiment for maintaining a calm starting system. Repeating behaviour in a stable context can support cue-driven habit formation; this lesson only asks you to make your next two attempts observable, not to prove a habit.

Practice: generate your two-attempt instruction

Use your Lesson 75 pair. This takes 5–10 minutes.

Close the loop

Copy the instruction into your record. Run it for two ordinary attempts; the next lesson audits whether you followed this exact sentence before reading outcomes.

Questions or a difficult real record? Ask your teacher agent and include your pair labels plus the sentence you wrote.

Background: Gardner, Lally & Wardle, “Making health habitual” (2012). The decision rule here is a practical course experiment, not a diagnostic tool.
