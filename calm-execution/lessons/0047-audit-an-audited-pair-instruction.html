Calm Execution · Lesson 47

Audit an audited-pair instruction

Before reading the next pair’s outcomes, check whether your exact instruction was actually followed—once per attempt.

The one skill

Put the instruction from Lesson 46 beside its two covered attempts. For each attempt, ask only: did I follow the stated cue, two-minute action, and one permitted move? Label it followed, not followed, or unclear.

If either label is not followed or unclear, repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue/start outcomes from this pair yet.

This is a practical experiment for keeping your small system readable. Repetition in stable contexts is relevant to cue-linked habits; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Two records here are not proof of a cause.

Your 8-minute exercise

  1. Copy the exact instruction you used for the last two ordinary attempts.
  2. For attempt 1, compare what happened with that sentence. Choose one label.
  3. Do the same for attempt 2. Do not fill gaps from memory.
  4. Use the checker. If both labels are followed, the pair is ready for the cue-then-start reading. Otherwise, repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts—no catch-up and no new change.

Pair audit

What counts as success?

An honest not followed or unclear is useful evidence, not a failure. The win is a readable next pair: the same small instruction, two ordinary attempts, and no urgency or punishment. Ask your teacher if you want help making your instruction easier to verify.

Source: Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Evidence adjusts the system; it never judges the person.
