Anchor One Reliable Cue

Choose one event that will tell you exactly when to begin—without waiting to feel ready.

Win for today: leave with one testable sentence: “After [event], I will [two-minute first action].”

Use an event, not a wish

“Work on my priority this morning” asks you to notice a stretch of time and decide again. An anchor is a specific event you can observe: after I put down my coffee, when I close my email triage, or after I sit at my desk.

observable event → prepared first object → two-minute first action → record started?

In habit research, a habit is described as an action triggered by a contextual cue associated with its performance. Repetition in a consistent context may reduce reliance on conscious attention or motivation. That does not guarantee this cue will work for you; treat the next few days as a small design test.

Source: Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012), “Making health habitual” (verified 2026-07-24).

Pick the smallest dependable anchor

  1. It happens: choose an event that occurs on most of your relevant days.
  2. You can notice it: describe what you see or do, not a clock range or a feeling.
  3. It is close to the action: when possible, use an event near the place or device where the prepared first step is available.

Do not add a second cue, a reminder campaign, or a longer session. You are testing one link in the existing ritual.
