Calm Execution · Lesson 35

Write a maintenance handoff

Turn a six-record decision into one calm instruction for the next two ordinary attempts.

The one skill

A review is only useful if it hands off to a small next action. After your six-record continuity check, write one sentence that says what stays the same and when you will look again.

No earliest break repeated twice → keep the same written plan for the next two ordinary attempts → review those two records together.

If the set was incomplete, the handoff is still small: keep the current instruction and gather two ordinary records. If the same earliest break appeared twice, hand off to one already-specified bounded test—then make one ordinary attempt. No catch-up work is part of any handoff.

The cue-based practice in this course is informed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle’s review of habitual behaviour and contextual cues: verified source. The two-record handoff here is a practical course rule, not a scientific threshold.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Bring out the six records you just read. Mark the result: no repeated break, repeated cue/start-side break, or incomplete.
  2. Use the card below to write exactly one handoff. For maintenance or incomplete evidence, name the existing cue and two-minute action; do not improve them today.
  3. Put the sentence beside your log. Make the next ordinary attempt when its cue occurs. Stop after writing the handoff—planning more is not the exercise.

Interactive handoff card

What counts as complete

A maintenance handoff protects the mission because it makes beginning the next important action ordinary again. It is not a promise to be perfect, proof that the plan works forever, or permission to raise the workload. Ask your teacher if your records cannot be labelled without guessing.
