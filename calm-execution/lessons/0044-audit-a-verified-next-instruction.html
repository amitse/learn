Calm Execution · Lesson 44

Audit a verified next instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact sentence from Lesson 43 was actually used.

The one skill

Your instruction named one next move, a stable cue, and an exact two-minute action. An audit asks only whether each of its two covered attempts followed that sentence.

Read the sentence → audit attempt 1 → audit attempt 2 → choose the next review step.

This is a practical evidence exercise, not a claim that two attempts establish a cause. The course’s cue-focused planning is informed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle, “Making health habitual” (2012), which discusses cue-linked habits.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Put your Lesson 43 instruction beside its two covered records.
  2. For each record, compare the stated next move, cue, and two-minute action with what happened. Mark followed, not followed, or unclear.
  3. Use the checker below to write the calm next step.
  4. If either record is not followed or unclear, repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue/start outcomes yet.

Two-attempt audit

What counts as success?

A readable audit is success. If both attempts followed the sentence, you have a verified pair ready for cue/start triage. If not, the useful next action is simply to repeat the same instruction—not to improve your explanation. Ask your teacher if you want help making an instruction easier to audit.

Source: Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). The aim is evidence about the plan, never a verdict about you.
