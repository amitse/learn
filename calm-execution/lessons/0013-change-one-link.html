Calm Execution · Lesson 13

Change one link

Make the next attempt a readable experiment, not a redesign.

One skill

When you adjust your ritual, keep its other links exactly the same and test one named change once.

Your existing ritual has a primary cue, a prepared two-minute action, a finish line, and one fallback. A system adjustment changes one of those links. A redesign changes several at once, so tomorrow’s record cannot tell you what helped.

Repeated behaviour in stable contexts is relevant to habit learning; that is why this lesson uses stability as a practical experiment, not a promise about results. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

The boundary

One-link adjustment: “Keep my cue and action; place the document on my desk before the cue.”

Accidental redesign: “Use a new morning alarm, choose a new task, work for 25 minutes, and block every app.”

The first gives you useful evidence. The second is not wrong—it is simply too many experiments for one ordinary start.

10-minute applied exercise

Name → hold steady → test once

  1. Copy the plan into your start record.
  2. Run it once at your usual primary cue.
  3. Record the usual evidence: cue? · setup? · started? · finish line? · stop/continue.

Tomorrow’s rule

Do not add a second change after making this card. If the attempt misses, later name the earliest break and choose the next single test. If it starts, you may stop at the finish line; a continuation remains optional.

Ask your teacher: “Which parts of this plan changed, and can I reduce it to one test?”
