Calm Execution · Lesson 14

Write one obstacle–response rule

Decide one calm response before a predictable obstacle asks you to improvise.

One skill

For one predictable obstacle, write: “If [observable obstacle], then [same small response].”

Keep your primary cue, two-minute action, and finish line unchanged. This is not a second routine; it is one pre-decided response that protects the existing routine when a known obstacle appears.

Habit research distinguishes automatic, cue-triggered responses from merely frequent behaviour. Here, use an if–then rule as a practical way to make one response unambiguous—not as a guarantee that it will become automatic. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Make the obstacle observable

Useful: “If my meeting runs past my primary cue, then at my existing fallback cue I open the brief and write one bullet.”

Too vague: “If I feel unmotivated, then try harder.” Feelings are real, but they are difficult to recognize consistently and the response adds pressure.

Choose one event you can notice. The response should reuse your established two-minute action or your single fallback cue—not add reminders, punishment, or catch-up work.

10-minute applied exercise

Notice → respond → record

  1. Write the checked rule beside your existing start record.
  2. Use it only if that specific obstacle occurs on one ordinary attempt.
  3. Record: obstacle occurred? · response used? · started?, plus your usual fields.

Tomorrow’s boundary

If the obstacle does not happen, do your ordinary primary-cue ritual; do not manufacture a test. If it happens and the response does not help, review the earliest break later and change only this rule—or make no change—on the next attempt.

Ask your teacher: “Is this an observable obstacle, or have I accidentally created a second routine?”
