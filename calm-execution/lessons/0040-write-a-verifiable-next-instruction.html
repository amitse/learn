Calm Execution · Lesson 40

Write a verifiable next instruction

Turn a two-record triage into one sentence that tomorrow’s record can actually check.

The one skill

Write the next instruction so that it names what happens next and what stays stable. A future review should not need to guess what you meant.

One next move + the stable baseline + a two-attempt boundary.

Use one of three sentence shapes: Repeat [same instruction] for two ordinary attempts. · Keep [cue], [two-minute action], [frequency], and [active test] unchanged for two ordinary attempts. · On the next ordinary attempt, use [one exact already-specified test]; keep [the other links] stable.

The course’s cue-based framing is informed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle’s review of cue-triggered habits: source. The sentence rule here is a practical clarity exercise, not a claim that two records establish a cause.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Put the verified result from Lesson 39 beside your two records.
  2. Choose the matching branch below: incomplete, retain, cue twice, or start-side twice.
  3. Name the existing cue and two-minute action exactly as you use them. If a test is active, name it exactly; otherwise write “no active test.”
  4. Copy the generated sentence beside the records. Make the next ordinary attempt—no catch-up work and no extra change.

Interactive instruction writer

Good enough

If fields are unclear, your whole instruction can be: “Repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts and record readable fields.” That is a complete next move. Ask your teacher if your cue or action cannot fit in one observable sentence.

A clear instruction is not stricter. It simply gives the next two records an honest plan to compare.
