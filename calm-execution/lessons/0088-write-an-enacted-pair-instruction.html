Calm Execution · Lesson 88

Write an enacted-pair instruction

You have classified two attempts that followed the same plan. Now turn that evidence into one small sentence for the next two ordinary attempts.

One sentence = one move + the same cue + the exact two-minute action + two attempts.

If the pair was mixed, workable, or unclear: retain and gather. If both labels were the same readable cue or start-side break and you already have a matching test written: use that one test. Nothing else changes.

This is a practical method for keeping the experiment legible, not a claim that two records prove a cause. Repetition in a stable context is associated with behaviour becoming more cue-linked; keeping the cue and small action explicit lets you inspect one routine without adding pressure. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Your 10-minute exercise

Look at the pair you just classified. Choose the supported move, then write the next instruction. Use it for two ordinary attempts—not as a demand to do more work.

Do not invent a test today. The cue and action should be concrete enough that you can later mark each attempt followed, not followed, or unclear.

Boundary: stopping after the two-minute action is valid. No catch-up work, guilt, or extra change is required.

Next: audit whether the sentence was actually followed in both attempts. Ask your teacher if the pair, the existing test, or the wording is unclear.
