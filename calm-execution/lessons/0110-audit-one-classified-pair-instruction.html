Calm Execution · Lesson 110 · 10 minutes

Audit one classified-pair instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact next instruction was actually used.

The one skill

Bring forward the one sentence you wrote after your classified pair. Compare it with each of the two ordinary attempts it covered. Do not decide whether the attempts “worked” yet.

For each attempt: followed, not followed, or unclear.

Followed means the written move, the same observable cue, and the exact two-minute action were all used. Not followed means one is known to differ. Unclear means the record cannot tell. If either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. If both were followed, classify outcomes cue first, then start.

This is a practical evidence rule, not a judgement about effort. Stable-context repetition is a useful background for cue-based practice; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — audit your pair

Spend 5–10 minutes with your Lesson 109 instruction and its two covered records. Make the labels first; the tool gives only the next evidence step.

Instant check

Attempt 1 is followed. Attempt 2 is unclear. What comes next?

No urgency, catch-up work, or explanation of character is needed. A readable instruction comes before an outcome interpretation. Ask your teacher if “followed” versus “unclear” is difficult to decide.
