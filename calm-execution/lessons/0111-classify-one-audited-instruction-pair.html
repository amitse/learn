The one skill

Use this only after Lesson 110’s audit says followed for both covered attempts. For each attempt, read the record in the same order:

Did the cue occur? Then: did the exact two-minute action start?

Label it cue when the cue did not occur; start-side when the cue occurred but the action did not start; none when both happened; or unclear when the record cannot tell. These labels describe a record, not your character. If the same readable cue or start-side label appears twice, it may support the one already-specified matching bounded test. Otherwise, retain and gather two ordinary records.

This is a practical reading rule for your own records. Stable-context repetition is relevant background for cue-based practice; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — classify your audited pair

Spend 5–10 minutes with the two records that both passed the enactment audit. For each attempt, answer from the record—not from how the day felt.

Instant check

Both audited attempts show cue occurred: yes and two-minute action started: no. Which label and next move fit?

No urgency, catch-up work, or self-punishment is required. Next, turn this classified pair into one checkable instruction. Ask your teacher if a record feels ambiguous.
