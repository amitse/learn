Calm Execution · Lesson 09

Name the two-minute finish line

Make the opening step have a visible ending, so starting does not immediately become another decision.

One skill

At your usual cue, do the same prepared action until one observable condition is true—then stop or freely continue.

Your cue, setup, fallback, and one blocked detour stay exactly as they are. Today only adds an end marker to the two-minute opening action.

This is a practical design experiment, not a demand to do more. Repeating an action in a stable context can help make it cue-driven over time; this lesson keeps that context simple and inspectable. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

What counts as a finish line?

It describes a result you can see, not a feeling or a larger task:

  • “The document is open and I have written three rough bullet points.”
  • “The notebook is open and I have copied the next problem.”
  • “The project is open and I have run the existing test once.”

“Feel ready,” “make progress,” and “work for a while” are not finish lines: they leave the ending open for negotiation.

10-minute applied exercise

Plan → check → trial

  1. Do the one-barrier setup you already chose.
  2. At the cue (or your one fallback if the primary cue was missed), block the same detour and begin.
  3. Stop when the visible finish line is true. Record: finish line reached? y/n and started? y/n.

Tomorrow’s rule

Use this finish line for one attempt only. If it was unclear or too large, change only the finish line after the attempt. Do not compensate with extra time, a longer session, or self-criticism.

Ask your teacher: “Is this finish line observable enough?” Bring your exact sentence and what happened at the cue.
