Calm Execution · Lesson 79 · 10 minutes

Write a classified-pair instruction

Turn an audit-passed pair into one small, checkable next move—without making the plan bigger.

One instruction has four parts: one next move, the same observable cue, the exact two-minute action, and a two-attempt boundary.

The one skill

  1. Use only a pair that passed the enactment audit: both attempts were followed.
  2. Read the two cue-first labels from Lesson 78.
  3. If the same readable break is cue or start-side twice, write one already-specified matching test. Keep every other link stable.
  4. For none, mixed, or unclear labels, write retain and gather: keep the current plan and collect two ordinary records.
  5. Make the boundary visible: this instruction covers the next two ordinary attempts only. It is not a verdict and needs no catch-up work.

This is a practical course experiment for making the next attempt inspectable. It does not diagnose why a start did or did not happen.

Practice: generate your next instruction

Allow 5–10 minutes. Start with a pair that has two followed audit labels.

Close the loop

Copy the sentence into your start log. After two ordinary attempts, audit whether you followed that exact sentence before interpreting the outcome. Questions or a difficult record? Ask your teacher agent and include the two labels plus your drafted instruction.

Background reading: Gardner, Lally & Wardle, “Making health habitual” (2012). The decision rule on this page is a practical course exercise.
