Calm Execution · Lesson 73

Write a checkable next instruction

Your last step classified two followed attempts. Now turn that reading into one sentence that can be enacted—and audited—over the next two ordinary attempts.

One skill

Write a two-attempt instruction with a visible next move and a stable baseline. It is not a promise to be productive; it is a small instruction whose enactment can be checked later.

For the next two ordinary attempts, [retain and gather / use one named existing test]. After [observable cue], [exact two-minute action].

Choose the branch from your classified pair

  1. If the pair was none, mixed, or unclear, choose retain and gather.
  2. If both labels were the same readable break and an already-specified matching test exists, choose that one test.
  3. Do not invent a new test here. Do not add duration, catch-up work, or a second change.

This is a practical decision rule for keeping your personal experiment readable, not a claim that two records establish a cause.

10-minute applied exercise

Set a ten-minute timer. Read your last classified pair, write the instruction below, then run the checklist. This becomes the sentence you will compare against the next two attempts.

After you write it

Place the sentence beside your start record. For each of the next two attempts, first note whether the sentence was followed, not followed, or unclear. Only a pair of followed attempts is ready for the familiar cue-then-start reading.

Context: repeated behavior in stable contexts is discussed as a basis for habit formation in Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Today’s exercise is deliberately smaller: make one chosen next action clear enough to enact and inspect.

Questions or a messy real example? Ask your agent-teacher. A useful next instruction is allowed to be plain.
