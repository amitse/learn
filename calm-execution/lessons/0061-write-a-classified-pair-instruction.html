Calm Execution · Lesson 61

Write a classified-pair instruction

Turn two verified labels into one small, auditable next move—without redesigning the whole ritual.

8–12 minutes

One skill

Write one exact instruction for the next two ordinary attempts, based only on your Lesson 60 pair classification.

Decision rule: two matching readable cue labels may use one already-specified cue test. Two matching readable start-side labels may use one already-specified setup/opening test. All other pairs mean: retain the current plan and gather two readable attempts.

The practical design keeps a stable context and a small repeated action; these are relevant features of cue-dependent habit formation discussed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). This lesson is a planning exercise, not a claim that one pair proves a cause.

Real-world exercise

  1. Put your two Lesson 60 labels beside you. If either is unclear, treat the result as retain and gather.
  2. Use the planner below to write one instruction. Name the existing cue and exact two-minute action; select only a test you had already specified, if the pair supports one.
  3. Copy the resulting sentence into your normal log. Follow it for the next two ordinary attempts—no catch-up work and no extra change. This takes 8–12 minutes.

Instruction planner

Finish calmly

The sentence is a temporary instruction, not a verdict. Next time, audit whether it was followed before interpreting cue or start outcomes. Ask your teacher: “Here is my two-attempt instruction—does it preserve one change and remain auditable?”
