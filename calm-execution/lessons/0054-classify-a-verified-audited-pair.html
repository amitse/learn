One skill: name the earliest readable break

Use this only after Lesson 53’s audit gave followed for both attempts. For each record, ask: did the cue occur? If yes, did the exact two-minute action start?

cue = the cue did not occur.
start-side = the cue occurred, but the two-minute action did not start.
none = both occurred and the action started.
unclear = the record cannot tell you.

This is a practical reading rule, not a verdict. Only two matching readable cue or start-side labels support using one already-specified matching test. Every other pair keeps the current small plan for two ordinary attempts.

8-minute applied exercise

  1. Put the exact instruction from Lesson 52 beside the two attempts that passed your Lesson 53 audit.
  2. For each attempt, read the recorded cue before the recorded start. Choose unclear rather than filling a gap from memory.
  3. Use the checker to write one next move: retain and gather two ordinary records, or use one already-specified matching test. Keep the cue, exact two-minute action, and frequency stable.

Why keep the context stable?

The supporting habit review distinguishes behaviour repeated in a stable context from automatic cue-triggered habit. Here, stable cue and action details make your own small records easier to compare; this lesson does not claim two records establish a cause. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012), “Making health habitual”.

No catch-up work, guilt, or longer session is required. Your task is to make the next ordinary attempt readable. Ask your teacher if a cue or start field is hard to classify.
