Calm Execution · Lesson 19

Write a maintenance rule

When your cue and two-minute start are working, the next skill is not an upgrade. It is knowing how to leave a workable plan alone.

One skill

Use two working carry-forward records to write one rule: keep the same cue, opening action, and frequency for the next two ordinary attempts.

This is a practical maintenance experiment, not a claim that the ritual is permanent. It protects a readable plan from unnecessary redesign. If a later record shows an earliest break, return to the existing one-link review.

Why “no change” can be a decision

Habit research distinguishes cue-triggered behaviour from behaviour that is merely frequent, and discusses repetition in stable contexts. That does not guarantee that a plan will become automatic. Here, your own two records are simply enough evidence to test stability before adding complexity.

Background: Gardner, Lally & Wardle, “Making health habitual.” Verified open-access review.

5–10 minutes

Applied exercise: make a two-attempt maintenance card

  1. Look at two recent carry-forward records.
  2. If each says cue occurred: yes and two-minute action started: yes, copy the same cue and action onto a small card.
  3. Write: “For my next two ordinary attempts, I will keep this plan unchanged.”
  4. Place the card by the object used for the opening action. Make the next attempt normally—no longer session, bonus task, or catch-up work.
Example: “After I close lunch, I open the outline and write its title for two minutes. For my next two ordinary attempts, I will keep this plan unchanged.”

Build and check your rule

Immediate feedback only; nothing is stored.

Tomorrow’s rule

If both links worked twice, retain the plan. If they did not, do not force maintenance—read the earliest break and test one small link instead.

Ask your teacher to help read a confusing record. The aim remains a calm, intentional beginning—not urgency, guilt, punishment, or a bigger system.
