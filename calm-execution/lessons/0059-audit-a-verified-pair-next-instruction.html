Calm Execution · Lesson 59

Audit a verified-pair next instruction

Before interpreting two attempts, check whether the exact instruction you wrote was actually the one you used.

8–12 minutes

One skill

Compare one written instruction with each of the two ordinary attempts it covered, then label each attempt followed, not followed, or unclear.

This is a practical evidence rule, not a claim that two attempts prove a cause. If either label is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue/start results yet; do not add a change.

Context: this course keeps the cue explicit and the action small. For background on cue-dependent habits in stable contexts, see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Real-world exercise

  1. Copy your exact Lesson 58 instruction where you can see it. It should name the cue, exact two-minute action, and one next move.
  2. Take the two ordinary attempts it covered. For each one, ask only: “Did I follow that sentence?” Choose a label below; do not reconstruct missing details.
  3. Use the result: two followed labels unlock the existing cue-then-start reading. Any other result means repeat the same sentence for two ordinary attempts—no catch-up, longer session, or redesign.

Two-attempt audit

Finish calmly

“Unclear” is a useful label, not a failed one. Your job is only to make the next two attempts readable. Ask your teacher: “Here is my instruction and audit. Should I repeat it, or is the pair ready for cue/start classification?”
