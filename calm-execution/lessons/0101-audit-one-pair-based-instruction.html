Calm Execution · Lesson 101 · 8 minutes

Audit one pair-based instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether you actually used the exact instruction in its two covered attempts.

The one skill

Put the instruction from Lesson 100 beside its two records. For each attempt, mark:

  • followed — the one stated move, cue, exact two-minute action, and boundary were used;
  • not followed — one of those facts was not used; or
  • unclear — the record cannot tell.

If either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue or start results yet.

Why audit first?

This is a practical reading rule: an instruction that was not enacted cannot yet be tested. Keeping the context and action explicit is consistent with the distinction between cue-driven habits and mere repetition described by Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — audit two attempts

Take 5–10 minutes. Copy the sentence you wrote in Lesson 100. Read only whether each attempt matched it—not whether it was productive.

Instant check

Attempt 1 followed the instruction. Attempt 2 is unclear. What comes next?

Ask your teacher if “followed” is hard to decide. The aim is readable evidence, not urgency, guilt, punishment, or catch-up work.
