Audit an audited-pair instruction

Before reading whether the cue or start worked, check whether you actually used the same instruction twice.

The one skill

Use the exact instruction you made in Lesson 97. Compare it with each of its two covered attempts—not with your intention or the outcome.

  • Followed: the stated move, cue, and exact two-minute action were used.
  • Not followed: a stated part was not used.
  • Unclear: the record cannot establish it.

If either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret outcomes or add a change yet.

Why this order?

This is a practical evidence-reading rule: keeping the written plan stable makes the next records easier to compare. Repetition in a stable context is also central to the distinction between cue-driven habits and mere frequency. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).
