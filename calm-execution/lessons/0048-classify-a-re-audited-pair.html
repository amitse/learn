Calm Execution · Lesson 48

Classify a re-audited pair

Once both attempts followed the instruction, read the outcomes in one calm order: cue, then start.

The one skill

Use this only after the Lesson 47 audit says both attempts were followed. For each record, label the earliest readable break: cue (the cue did not occur), start-side (cue occurred but the two-minute action did not start), none (both occurred), or unclear.

Retain the current plan and gather two ordinary records unless the same readable break appears twice and an already-specified matching test exists.

This is a practical reading rule, not proof of a cause. Repetition in stable contexts is relevant to cue-linked habits; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Your 8-minute exercise

  1. Take the two attempts that passed your instruction audit.
  2. For attempt 1, read cue occurred? before two-minute action started?. Choose its label.
  3. Repeat for attempt 2. If either field is missing, choose unclear; do not reconstruct it.
  4. Use the checker, then write only its next move. No catch-up, extra duration, or new change.

Pair classifier

What counts as success?

A clear label is enough. Unclear is honest evidence, and a mixed pair does not demand a redesign. The win is keeping one small, checkable start ritual free of urgency, guilt, and punishment. Ask your teacher if you want help distinguishing cue from start-side.

Source: Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Evidence adjusts the system; it never judges the person.
