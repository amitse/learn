Calm Execution · Lesson 108 · 8 minutes

Classify one audit-passed pair

Once both attempts followed the plan, locate the first break—without turning it into a verdict.

The one skill

Use this only after Lesson 107’s audit says followed for both covered attempts. For each attempt, read two facts in the same order:

1. Did the cue occur?   2. Did the exact two-minute action start?

Then assign one label: cue (cue did not occur), start-side (cue occurred; action did not start), none (both occurred), or unclear (a fact is missing).

This is a practical reading rule for your own records. Keep the cue and action named; stable-context repetition is useful background for cue–response practice (Gardner, Lally & Wardle, 2012).

Applied exercise — classify your pair

Spend 5–10 minutes with the two records that passed your enactment audit. Record only what is written. Do not explain why.

Instant check

Both attempts passed the audit. Attempt 1: cue yes, action no. Attempt 2: cue yes, action no. Which pair label is correct?

Two matching readable breaks are evidence to consult your already-specified matching test—not a reason to invent a new one today. Mixed, working, or unclear pairs mean retain the plan and gather two ordinary records. Ask your teacher if a label is hard to assign. No guilt, punishment, catch-up work, or expansion is needed.
