Calm Execution · Lesson 16

Run a 30-Day Progress Check

Use the record to choose the next calm experiment—not to grade yourself or create catch-up work.

One skill: make a count useful

Your mission names 20 intentional starts in 30 days. A count is helpful only if it leads to a clear next move. It is not a streak, a verdict, or a reason to make today’s opening step bigger.

Count starts → read one recurring link → retain the ritual or test one named change.

The course’s cue-based design is consistent with a review of habit research describing habits as cue-triggered responses learned in a stable context. That does not make this check a guarantee; treat your own record as the evidence for your system. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012)

The 10-minute check

  1. Count: read up to 30 daily records and count each started? yes. A fallback start counts. Stopping at the two-minute finish line still counts.
  2. Read: look only for the earliest repeated break: cue, setup, action, or obstacle-response use. “No clear pattern” is an allowed result.
  3. Decide: retain the current ritual if it is readable or the pattern is unclear. Otherwise name one small link to test on the next ordinary attempt.

Do not: compensate for a low count, add reminders, add a second routine, or lengthen the two-minute baseline.

Practice now · 5–20 minutes

Open your existing start record. Complete this compact decision card, then make the next attempt exactly as planned.

What success looks like

A completed check produces one sentence and preserves the ordinary start ritual. The mission is reliable beginning without urgency, guilt, or punishment—not a perfect number.

Bring your decision or a confusing record back to your teacher for a calm review.
