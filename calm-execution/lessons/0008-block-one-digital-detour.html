Block One Digital Detour

Give the existing two-minute start a clear path by making one predictable detour unavailable—briefly.

Win for today: choose one digital detour and one reversible block that lasts only through your two-minute opening action.

Protect the doorway, not the whole day

At the cue, you do not need a perfect attention system. You need the already-chosen opening action to be easier to reach than one familiar detour: perhaps a news tab, inbox, chat app, or social feed. Test a single, temporary block—close the tab, silence one app, put the phone across the room, or use a site blocker for two minutes.

cue → one detour unavailable → same prepared two-minute action → record

This is a practical experiment, not a promise that blocking a distraction will solve procrastination. Research on habit distinguishes behaviours prompted by stable contexts from merely frequent behaviours; that is a reason to preserve your primary cue and tiny action while you test one environmental change (Gardner, Lally & Wardle, 2012; verified 2026-07-28).

The one-detour rule

  1. Keep your primary cue, fallback rule, setup, and two-minute action exactly as they are.
  2. Name the first digital detour you usually take at the cue—not every distraction.
  3. Choose one reversible block that takes under a minute and lasts only until the two-minute action begins.
  4. After the attempt, record detour blocked? y/n — started? y/n. If it did not help, change one thing tomorrow; do not add a larger restriction today.

Make a two-minute protection card

Choose an action you can actually reverse. The card is a test, not a ban.

Quick check

Which plan follows the one-detour rule?

10-minute field exercise

  1. Minutes 0–2: Read your current primary cue and exact two-minute action. Leave both unchanged.
  2. Minutes 2–4: Identify the first digital detour you expect at that cue. Pick only one.
  3. Minutes 4–6: Set up one reversible block now: close the tab, move the phone, silence one notification source, or configure a two-minute site block.
  4. Minutes 6–8: Complete the protection card and place its rule beside your existing record.
  5. Minutes 8–10: At the next cue, use the block and begin the same action for two minutes. Immediately log cue? y/n — setup? y/n — detour blocked? y/n — started? y/n. A “no” is system data, not a failure.
