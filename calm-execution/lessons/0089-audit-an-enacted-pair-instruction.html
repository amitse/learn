Calm Execution · Lesson 89

Audit an enacted-pair instruction

Before reading whether the cue or start worked, check whether the exact Lesson 88 sentence was used in both ordinary attempts.

Compare the written sentence—not effort or outcome—with each attempt.

Mark each attempt followed, not followed, or unclear. If either is not followed or unclear, repeat the same sentence for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue/start results or add a change yet. Only two followed marks open the next cue-first reading.

This is a practical evidence rule, not a claim that two attempts establish a cause. Keeping a routine tied to a stable context can make it easier to inspect the cue and response separately; this exercise keeps that inspection small and explicit. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Your 10-minute real-world exercise

Put your exact Lesson 88 instruction beside the two attempts it covered. For each attempt, ask only: did the cue, exact two-minute action, and stated move match the sentence? Choose one label below for each. Then use the immediate feedback to write your next move on your paper log.

Boundary: “Not followed” and “unclear” are not failures. They mean the next useful move is readable enactment: repeat the same small instruction, with no catch-up work, guilt, or redesign.

Next: if both attempts were followed, classify each outcome cue first and start second. Ask your teacher if the exact sentence or either label is unclear.
