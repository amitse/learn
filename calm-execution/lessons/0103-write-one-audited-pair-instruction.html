Calm Execution · Lesson 103 · 10 minutes

Write one audited-pair instruction

Turn a readable pair into one small next move—without turning a miss into a verdict or a redesign.

The one skill

Use this only after both covered attempts were audited as followed and classified cue-first. Your next instruction has four parts:

  1. One move: retain and gather, or one already-specified matching test.
  2. The same observable cue.
  3. The exact two-minute action.
  4. A two-attempt boundary.

Mixed, workable, or unclear pairs get “retain and gather.” Only the same readable cue or start-side break twice can use one matching test that you already specified.

Why this narrow format?

This is a practical experiment: keeping the cue and action explicit makes the next attempt inspectable rather than relying on a feeling of readiness. Research on implementation intentions describes if–then plans as linking a specified situation with a response; see the verified Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis. For background on stable-context, cue-dependent habits, see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — make your next two-attempt card

Take 5–10 minutes. Use the two labels from Lesson 102. Do not solve a new problem here; write the smallest instruction the pair supports.

Instant check

Both audited attempts are labelled none. Which instruction fits?

After two ordinary attempts, audit whether this exact instruction was followed before reading outcomes. Ask your teacher if the pair or the wording is ambiguous. “Unclear” is data—not a reason for guilt, punishment, or catch-up work.
