Calm Execution · Lesson 100 · 8 minutes

Write one pair-based next instruction

Turn an audit-passed, classified pair into one small instruction you can actually follow twice.

The one skill

Use this only after both covered attempts were followed and Lesson 99 gave each one a label.

  1. If the pair is mixed, working, or unclear: retain and gather.
  2. If both readable labels are the same cue or start-side, use one already-specified matching test—and nothing else.

Every instruction names one move, the existing observable cue, the exact two-minute action, and a two-attempt boundary.

Why this narrow format?

This is a practical constraint, not a verdict about you: keeping the cue and action explicit makes the next two records easier to read. Repeated behaviour in a stable context is central to the distinction between cue-driven habits and mere frequency. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — make your next instruction

Take 5–10 minutes. Copy the cue and exact action from your current plan; do not improve them today. Then use the two labels from your audit-passed pair.

Instant check

Your pair labels are none and unclear. What is the next move?

Ask your teacher if the pair or its existing test is hard to name. The target is a readable next attempt—not urgency, guilt, punishment, or catch-up work.
