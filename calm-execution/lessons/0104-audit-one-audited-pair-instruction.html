Calm Execution · Lesson 104 · 10 minutes

Audit one audited-pair instruction

Before judging the two attempts, check whether you actually used the instruction you wrote.

The one skill

Put your exact Lesson 103 sentence beside the two attempts it covered. For each attempt, compare four things:

  1. the one move (retain/gather or the named existing test),
  2. the observable cue,
  3. the exact two-minute action, and
  4. the two-attempt boundary.

Label an attempt followed only when the instruction was enacted. If either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret cue or start outcomes yet.

Why keep this gate?

This is a practical measurement rule, not a verdict about effort: an outcome cannot tell you much about a plan that was not clearly enacted. Keeping the same context and response explicit is also consistent with the cue–response framing discussed in Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — audit the pair

Take 5–10 minutes. Read your written instruction first; do not look at whether you started until after this check.

Instant check

One attempt is followed; the other is unclear. What comes next?

When both attempts are followed, then—and only then—read outcomes cue first, start second. Ask your teacher if the instruction or an audit label is ambiguous. “Unclear” is usable data, never a reason for guilt, punishment, or catch-up work.
