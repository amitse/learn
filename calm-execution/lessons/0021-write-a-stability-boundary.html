Calm Execution · Lesson 21

Write a stability boundary

Decide in advance when a pattern is clear enough to test one revision—and when variation deserves no new complexity.

One skill

Revise one link only when the same earliest break appears in at least two readable records. Otherwise, keep the current plan for two ordinary attempts.

This is a practical experiment for protecting a small ritual from constant tinkering, not a scientific cutoff. Repetition in a stable context is relevant to habit practice, but your records—not a universal number—guide this boundary. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

8–12 minutes

Applied exercise: make your boundary card

  1. Read your latest four carry-forward records in order: cue, then start.
  2. Circle the earliest break in each readable miss: cue, or setup/opening action.
  3. Write this boundary somewhere visible: “I revise one link only after the same earliest break appears twice. Until then, I keep my plan for two ordinary attempts.”
  4. If one break meets that boundary, name one small test for that link only. If it does not, schedule two ordinary attempts with the plan unchanged.
Example: Two readable records missed at the cue; one had a successful start; one is incomplete. The cue is the repeated earliest break. Test one cue adjustment once—such as placing the cue note by the kettle. Keep the two-minute action and frequency unchanged.

Check your boundary decision

Immediate feedback only; nothing is stored.

Tomorrow’s rule

A repeated, same earliest break earns one small test. Mixed, isolated, incomplete, or working records earn restraint.

Ask your teacher to help classify a confusing record. The aim remains a calm, intentional beginning—not urgency, guilt, punishment, or a bigger system.
