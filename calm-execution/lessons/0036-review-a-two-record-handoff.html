Calm Execution · Lesson 36

Review a two-record handoff

Use the two ordinary attempts named in your handoff to choose the next small instruction—without escalating the plan.

The one skill

A handoff asks for two ordinary attempts. Now read those attempts in the same order every time: first, was any current test used? Then, did the cue occur? Finally, did the two-minute action start?

Two readable records with no repeated earliest break → retain the current instruction for two more ordinary attempts → review again.

If either record is incomplete, keep the instruction and gather two more records. If the same earliest break appears in both readable records, return to the existing boundary: specify and run one matching bounded test only. This is a practical course rule for keeping a small experiment interpretable, not a scientific threshold.

This course’s cue-based practice is informed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle’s review of habitual behaviour and contextual cues: verified source.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Place the two records named by your latest handoff side by side.
  2. For each, check test use (if a test is active), then cue, then start. Mark any unclear field incomplete; do not fill it in from memory.
  3. Use the decision card below. Write its one sentence beside your log, then stop. Your next action is one ordinary attempt at its usual cue—not catch-up work.

Interactive decision card

What counts as a good review

Good is not two perfect records. Good is an honest reading that produces one small next instruction. If the records do not answer the question, “gather two more ordinary records” is a complete decision. Ask your teacher if you cannot identify the fields without guessing.

Keep the existing frequency, cue, and two-minute action unless the evidence supports exactly one bounded test.
