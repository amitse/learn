Calm Execution · Lesson 78 · 10 minutes

Classify an audited instruction pair

Once both attempts followed the written instruction, locate the earliest observable break—without turning either result into a verdict.

Read each record in one order: cue occurred? first; then two-minute action started?. Label it cue, start-side, none, or unclear.

The one skill

  1. Use only the two attempts that passed Lesson 77’s audit: both must be followed.
  2. If the cue did not occur, label that record cue. Stop there; do not infer a start-side problem.
  3. If the cue occurred but the two-minute action did not start, label it start-side.
  4. If both occurred, label it none. If the record cannot establish the order, label it unclear.
  5. Only when the same readable break appears twice may you use one already-specified matching test. Otherwise, retain the plan and gather two more ordinary records.

This is a practical course experiment: it keeps attention on the first observable link, rather than adding urgency or self-blame.

Practice: classify your audited pair

Allow 5–10 minutes. First confirm both covered attempts were labelled followed in Lesson 77.

Close the loop

Write the two labels beside the records. Two matching cue or start-side labels support one already-specified matching test; every other result means retain the current instruction and gather two ordinary records. No catch-up work is needed.

Questions or a difficult record? Ask your teacher agent and include the two audit labels plus the cue/start evidence.

Background: Gardner, Lally & Wardle, “Making health habitual” (2012). The labels and decision rule are a practical course experiment, not a diagnostic tool.
