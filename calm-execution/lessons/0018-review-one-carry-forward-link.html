Calm Execution · Lesson 18

Review one carry-forward link

Your 30-day practice is closed. Now make the carry-forward plan inspectable without turning it into a new productivity project.

One skill

After a few carry-forward attempts, review one retained link: did the planned cue occur, and did you begin the two-minute action?

Do not redesign the whole ritual. Read the two facts, then choose either retain or test one small adjustment on the next ordinary attempt.

Why this is deliberately small

The previous lesson asked you to retain one useful component at a modest frequency. This is a practical experiment in keeping that plan readable: a stable context can matter for habit-like behaviour, but this lesson does not promise an outcome. It gives you a calm way to notice whether your chosen cue still opens the action.

Background: Gardner, Lally & Wardle’s review distinguishes habit from merely frequent behaviour and discusses repeated behaviour in stable contexts. Read the verified open-access review.

10–15 minutes

Applied exercise: make a two-record check

  1. Bring out two recent carry-forward records (or make two ordinary attempts before using this exercise).
  2. For each, mark only: cue occurred? and two-minute action started?
  3. Read in order. If the cue did not occur, inspect the cue. If it did but you did not start, inspect the smallest opening action or its setup.
  4. Choose one result: retain the plan, or write one tiny test for the next attempt. No catch-up work.
Example: “After I close lunch, I open the outline and write its title for two minutes.” Two records show lunch happened but the outline was not open. Test once: leave the outline open before lunch.

Build your one-link decision

This check gives immediate feedback. It stores nothing.

Tomorrow’s rule

Use the existing frequency and make one ordinary attempt. A review is for choosing the next test—not for earning back missed starts.

Questions or a record you want to read together? Ask your teacher. The goal remains one calm, intentional beginning—not urgency, guilt, or punishment.
