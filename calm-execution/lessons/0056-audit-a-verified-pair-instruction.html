Calm Execution · Lesson 56

Audit a verified-pair instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact sentence from Lesson 55 was actually used on each of its two ordinary attempts.

7–10 minutes

One skill

Label each covered attempt followed, not followed, or unclear by comparing it with your written instruction—not with what you meant to do.

This is a practical evidence check. If either attempt is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact sentence for two ordinary attempts. Do not diagnose the result, add a change, or catch up.

Repeated behaviour in a stable context is relevant to cue-based habit formation; here, keeping one written instruction stable is a modest practice design, not proof that it will work for you. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Real-world exercise

  1. Put your exact Lesson 55 instruction where you can see it. Take its two covered attempt records.
  2. For each attempt, ask only: did I use the stated cue, exact two-minute action, and the one stated move (retain/gather or the named test)?
  3. Choose a label below. Then follow the feedback: repeat unchanged unless both attempts are followed.

Two-attempt audit

Finish calmly

Both followed labels mean the pair is ready for the existing cue-then-start classification. Any other label means the next instruction is simply: “Repeat the exact Lesson 55 instruction for two ordinary attempts and record it readably.”

Ask your teacher: “Here is my instruction and my two records. Which audit label is supported by what I wrote?”
