Calm Execution · Lesson 87

Classify an enacted cue-first pair

Once two attempts really followed the same instruction, read what happened in a fixed order. This keeps a result from becoming a story about your character.

For each enacted attempt: cue first, start second.

  1. cue — the cue did not occur.
  2. start-side — the cue occurred, but the exact two-minute action did not begin.
  3. none — both the cue occurred and the action began.
  4. unclear — one of those facts was not recorded.

This is a practical reading rule, not a verdict. It preserves the small cue-action experiment. Repetition in a stable context is commonly discussed as a route by which behaviour can become more cue-linked; this lesson simply helps you keep the evidence legible rather than changing several parts at once. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Your 8-minute exercise

Take the two ordinary attempts covered by your current written instruction. First confirm both were marked followed. Then use the chooser for each attempt. Do not fill gaps from memory.

Decision boundary: two matching readable cue or start-side labels may use one already-specified matching test. Any mixed pair, none, or unclear means retain the current cue and exact two-minute action and gather two ordinary records. No catch-up work.

Next: turn the pair into one exact two-attempt instruction. Ask your teacher if a label or the boundary is unclear.
