Calm Execution · Lesson 64

Write a verified classified-pair instruction

You have two followed attempts and their outcome labels. Convert that small evidence set into one instruction you can actually enact twice.

One pair → one next move → the same cue and exact two-minute action.

One skill

Write a next instruction that matches—not exceeds—your pair classification:

  • Two matching readable breaks: use one already-specified matching test.
  • Anything else (none, mixed labels, or unclear): retain the plan and gather two ordinary readable attempts.

Your sentence must name the observable cue and exact two-minute action, then say what to do for the next two attempts. This is a practical planning exercise: stable contexts can support cue-driven repetition, but this tiny record is for making a calm local decision, not proving a personal trait. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Sentence shapes

  1. Retain / gather: “For the next two ordinary attempts, after [cue], I will [exact two-minute action]. I will record cue and start; no changes.”
  2. One matching test: “For the next two ordinary attempts, after [cue], I will [exact two-minute action] using [already-specified test]. I will record cue and start; no other changes.”

Do not invent a new test from this pair. If the matching test was never specified, use the retain / gather sentence instead.
