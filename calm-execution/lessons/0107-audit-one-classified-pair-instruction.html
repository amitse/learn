Calm Execution · Lesson 107 · 8 minutes

Audit one classified-pair instruction

Before reading results, check whether you actually used the sentence you wrote.

The one skill

Take the exact instruction from Lesson 106 and compare it with its two covered ordinary attempts. Give each attempt one label: followed, not followed, or unclear.

If both are followed, you may next read cue and start outcomes. If either is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not interpret outcomes yet.

This keeps a change from being confused with an untested plan. It is a practical record-keeping rule, not a judgment about effort.

Why keep cue and action named?

Stable-context repetition is relevant to cue–response accounts of habit; that is why the instruction retains an observable cue and a small action. See Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012), “Making health habitual”. Today’s audit is a practical experiment: record what happened before changing the system.

Applied exercise — audit the two attempts

Spend 5–10 minutes with your exact Lesson 106 sentence and its two records. Do not rate the quality of the work—only whether that sentence was enacted.

Instant check

Attempt 1 is followed; attempt 2 is unclear. What is the next move?

Keep the instruction unchanged when evidence is incomplete. That is not falling behind; it is making the next record readable. Ask your teacher if “followed” is hard to decide. No guilt, punishment, catch-up work, or expansion is needed.
