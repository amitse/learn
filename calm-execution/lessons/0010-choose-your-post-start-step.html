Calm Execution · Lesson 10

Choose your post-start step

After the finish line, make a small choice—not an automatic demand to keep going.

One skill

When your two-minute finish line is true, deliberately choose: stop, or do one pre-defined five-minute continuation.

Stopping counts. Continuing is an option you decide in advance, not a debt created by starting. Keep your cue, setup, fallback, detour block, and finish line unchanged.

This is a practical experiment in keeping the ritual bounded and repeatable. Repeating a behaviour in a stable context can make it more cue-driven over time; the point here is to protect that stable, small opening rather than demand a longer session. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

The choice has only two valid answers

  • Stop: record the start and leave without explaining or compensating.
  • Continue: do one named, five-minute next step, then stop or freely choose again.

“Keep working until I feel done” is not a continuation plan; it reopens the decision. Name one small action you can see yourself doing.

10-minute applied exercise

Pre-choose → try → record

  1. At your usual cue, use your existing ritual through the same two-minute finish line.
  2. Then press one choice below. Either choice is a successful use of the skill.

Tomorrow’s rule

Test the same two-option choice once. If continuing feels too large or vague, shrink only the five-minute step. Do not turn a stop into a miss, add catch-up work, or use guilt to extend the session.

Ask your teacher: “Is my continuation step concrete enough to choose calmly?” Bring the exact sentence and which option you chose.
