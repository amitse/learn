Calm Execution · Lesson 25

Write a bounded test specification

When your triage supports a test, make the change precise enough to keep the next record readable.

One skill

Write one sentence: test one link, name its exact change, and name what stays stable.

This is a practical experiment, not proof about why a start succeeds or fails. It preserves the course’s cue-based design; habit research distinguishes cue-triggered habits from merely frequent behaviour in stable contexts. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

8–12 minutes

Applied exercise: write tomorrow’s one-line test

  1. Look at your most recent triage. If no earliest break repeated twice, write “Retain: no test tomorrow.” That is a complete result.
  2. If cue repeated, choose one cue change. If start-side repeated, choose one: setup or opening action.
  3. Write: “Test [link]: [exact change]. Keep [stable links] unchanged for one ordinary attempt.”
  4. Place the sentence beside the existing record. Make one ordinary attempt; do not add a second change.
Example: “Test setup: place the outline on the desk after lunch. Keep the after-lunch cue and two-minute opening action unchanged for one ordinary attempt.”

Check your specification

Immediate feedback only; nothing is stored.

Tomorrow’s rule

No repeated break: retain. Repeated break: one named change, one ordinary attempt, stable remainder.

Ask your teacher to help turn a vague change into an observable sentence. The aim remains one calm intentional beginning—not urgency, guilt, punishment, or a larger system.
