Calm Execution · Lesson 41

Audit a two-attempt instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact plan from Lesson 40 was actually followed.

The one skill

An instruction can only be compared with its records when you can tell whether each covered attempt followed it. Read the sentence first—not your memory of what you intended—then label each attempt followed, not followed, or unclear.

Instruction first → attempt 1 → attempt 2 → one calm next move.

If either label is not followed or unclear, do not interpret cue or start outcomes as a verdict on the plan. Repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts and make the record readable. This is a practical audit rule, not a claim that two records establish cause.

The course uses an observable cue and action because habits are commonly defined as actions triggered by contextual cues associated with their performance; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle, Making health habitual. The audit process here is a practical exercise.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Find your exact next-instruction sentence from Lesson 40 and the two attempts it covered.
  2. For each attempt, compare only what the instruction required: retain, gather readable records, or use one named test while keeping the stated cue and two-minute action stable.
  3. Choose a label for each attempt below. Do not fill gaps from memory.
  4. Copy the generated next move beside your records, then make the next two attempts ordinary—no catch-up work, added duration, or second change.

Interactive audit

Good enough

“Unclear” is a useful result. It means the next job is to repeat one readable instruction, not to explain yourself. Ask your teacher if it is hard to tell what the instruction required.

The goal is a checkable next attempt, not a perfect explanation of the last one.
