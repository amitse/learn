Calm Execution · Lesson 118 · 8 minutes

Audit one classified-pair instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact next instruction was actually used.

The one skill

Take the decision-ready sentence you wrote after your classified pair. Compare it with each of its two covered ordinary attempts. Give each attempt one label:

Followed = the named cue, exact two-minute action, and any named test were used.
Not followed = a named part was not used.
Unclear = the record cannot tell.

If both attempts are followed, their cue/start evidence is ready to classify next. If either is not followed or unclear, repeat the exact instruction for two ordinary attempts. This is a practical reading rule: do not make a new explanation or a new test from an instruction that was not yet readable. A stable context can support cue-linked repetition; see Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012) for background.

Applied exercise — audit the covered pair

Spend 5–10 minutes with your written instruction and its two records. Do not inspect whether you “succeeded” yet—only whether the instruction was enacted.

Instant check

One covered attempt is followed; the other is unclear. What is the next move?

Audit enactment first; interpret outcomes second. The aim is a usable next start, not a verdict. Ask your teacher if the instruction or either record is ambiguous. Next: classify cue and start evidence only after both attempts pass.
