Calm Execution · Lesson 116 · 8 minutes

Audit one decision-ready instruction

Before reading outcomes, check whether the exact instruction from Lesson 115 was actually used.

The one skill

Copy your exact decision-ready instruction. For each of its two covered ordinary attempts, compare the record with that sentence—not with what you meant to do.

Label each attempt followed, not followed, or unclear.
If either label is not followed, repeat the same instruction for two ordinary attempts. Do not classify cue or start outcomes yet.

This is a practical reading rule: it keeps a changed or incomplete attempt from being treated as evidence about the written plan. Stable contexts can make cue-based repetition easier to establish; this audit simply keeps the next comparison readable. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — audit the pair

Spend 5–10 minutes with your Lesson 115 sentence and its two records. First copy the sentence. Then give each attempt one enactment label. This is data, not a grade.

Instant check

Your labels are followed and unclear. What comes next?

First establish that the instruction was enacted; then, and only then, read cue and start outcomes. Ask your teacher if a record cannot show whether the instruction was followed. Next: classify an audit-passed pair cue first.
