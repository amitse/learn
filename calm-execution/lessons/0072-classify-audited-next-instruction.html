Calm Execution · Lesson 72

Classify an audited next instruction

Once two ordinary attempts truly followed the instruction, read what happened—without adding urgency, blame, or a new plan too soon.

One skill

Classify a verified pair in the same order each time: cue first, then start.

Do not interpret outcomes until both attempts are labelled followed.

  1. For each attempt, ask: Did the observable cue occur?
  2. If it occurred, ask: Did the exact two-minute action start?
  3. Label each record cue, start-side, none, or unclear.

Labels: cue = the cue did not occur; start-side = cue occurred but the action did not start; none = both occurred; unclear = the record cannot tell.

Decision boundary

Two matching, readable breaks can support one already-specified matching test. Otherwise retain the instruction and gather two more ordinary records. An unclear record is information to gather, not a failure to fix.

This is a practical evidence rule for this course, not a diagnostic claim. The course’s cue-oriented design is informed by the distinction between repeated behavior and cue-linked habit in Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012) (verified source).

10-minute applied exercise

Take the exact instruction from Lesson 70 and its two covered attempts. First confirm both are followed. Then enter the two outcome labels below. Keep your cue, two-minute action, and instruction unchanged while you make the classification.

Write the resulting one-line decision in your log: “For the next two ordinary attempts, I will …” No catch-up work.

Quick check

Both verified attempts show: cue occurred → action did not start. What is the label?

Start-side. It is readable twice; use only an already-specified matching test. If none exists, retain and gather rather than inventing several changes.
