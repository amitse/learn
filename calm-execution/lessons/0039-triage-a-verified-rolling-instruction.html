Calm Execution · Lesson 39

Triage a verified rolling instruction

Turn two faithfully recorded attempts into one calm next instruction—without redesigning the ritual.

The one skill

Once you have verified that the prior instruction was followed in both attempts, label each record at its earliest readable break: none, cue, or start-side. Then make just one next instruction.

Incomplete verification → repeat the same instruction. A matching break twice → use one already-specified matching test. Anything else → retain the plan for two ordinary attempts.

This is a practical decision rule for keeping your start ritual readable. It is not a claim that two records prove why a start did or did not happen. Its purpose is to prevent urgency-driven daily redesign while you practice beginning one important action.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Place your last written rolling instruction beside its two covered records.
  2. First verify it was followed in both. If not, write: Repeat the same instruction; gather two readable records.
  3. If it was followed, label each record: none (cue and start happened), cue (cue did not happen), or start-side (cue happened but the two-minute action did not start).
  4. Use the chooser below and copy its sentence beside the records. Make only the next ordinary attempt—no catch-up work.

Interactive rolling triage

Good enough

“Repeat the same instruction” and “retain the plan” are complete decisions. You are practicing reliable starts, not trying to win an argument with two lines of data. Ask your teacher if a cue or start field is too vague to label.

Verify first. Label the two records second. Then choose one small instruction that leaves the ritual calm and usable.
