Calm Execution · Lesson 46

Write the next audited-pair instruction

Turn your two labels into one instruction that you can calmly follow—and audit—over the next two ordinary attempts.

The one skill

After you classify an audited pair, write only the next move the pair supports. Name the stable cue and exact two-minute action so the next pair can be checked against the instruction.

Mixed, workable, or unclear pair → retain and gather two records. Same readable break twice + an already-specified matching test → run only that test.

Stable contexts can help behaviour become cue-linked over repetition, as reviewed by Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). The two-attempt instruction below is a practical course rule for keeping your experiment readable—not a claim that two records establish a cause.

Your 10-minute exercise

  1. Bring forward the two labels from Lesson 45: cue, start-side, none, or unclear.
  2. Choose the matching next move below.
  3. Write your existing cue and exact two-minute action. Keep them unchanged unless you are running one already-specified matching test.
  4. Use the sentence for two ordinary attempts. Record whether it was followed; do not add duration, catch-up work, or another change.

Instruction builder

What counts as success?

A specific, restrained instruction is success—even if it says “retain.” The target is a repeatable beginning, not an impressive plan. Ask your teacher for help making the cue or two-minute action observable.

Source: Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Evidence adjusts the system; it never judges the person.
