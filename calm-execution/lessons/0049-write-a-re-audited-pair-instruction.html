Calm Execution · Lesson 49

Write a re-audited pair instruction

Turn today’s pair classification into one small instruction that can be checked after two ordinary attempts.

One skill: make the next move auditable

Once both attempts have been audited and classified, do not redesign the ritual. Write one sentence for the next two attempts. It must say what to do and what stays stable.

For the next two ordinary attempts: [one next move]; after [stable cue], do [exact two-minute action]. Then audit whether I followed this sentence.

If the pair was unclear, none, or mixed, the next move is usually “retain and gather two readable attempts.” If both labels were the same readable break, use only the already-specified matching test. This is a practical guardrail against changing several links at once—not a verdict about you.

Why keep a cue stable? Repetition in a stable context is part of the habit-formation account reviewed by Gardner, Lally, and Wardle; this course uses that idea as a small, testable design choice rather than a promise of automaticity. Read the review.

10-minute applied exercise

  1. Take your latest audited pair. Write its two labels: cue, start-side, none, or unclear.
  2. Choose one move: retain/gather, or one already-specified matching test only if the same readable break appears twice.
  3. Write the sentence in the planner below. Copy it beside your start record.
  4. Use it for two ordinary attempts. At the next review, audit the sentence before interpreting outcomes.

Instruction check

Stopping after the two-minute action remains a valid start. Ask your teacher if your pair does not fit these branches or if you want help wording a matching test.
