Calm Execution · Lesson 97 · 10 minutes

Write an audited-pair instruction

Turn a classified, audit-passed pair into one small instruction you can actually check two times.

The one skill

Use this only after Lesson 96: both attempts were followed and you have labelled each one cue-first.

Write exactly one next move:

  • Retain and gather for a working, mixed, or unclear pair.
  • Use one already-specified matching test only when the same readable break appeared twice.

Name the same observable cue, the exact two-minute action, and a two-attempt boundary. Do not add a new test, output goal, or catch-up work.

Why keep it small?

This is a practical evidence-reading exercise, not a claim about your character. Repetition in a stable context is relevant to cue-linked habits; here, a narrow instruction lets you see whether the planned action was enacted before changing it again. Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012).

Applied exercise — make your next instruction

Spend 5–10 minutes with your Lesson 96 pair. Copy the cue and exact two-minute action already in your plan. Then choose the move supported by that pair.

Instant check

Both audited attempts were followed. One is none; one is start-side. What is the supported next instruction?

Ask your teacher if the pair or a proposed test is hard to read. The aim is a calm, checkable next attempt—not urgency, guilt, or a verdict.
