# Learning Record 0015 — Read One Obstacle–Response Record

## Date

2026-08-04

## Lesson

0015 — Read One Obstacle–Response Record

## What was taught

- The learner reads a single obstacle–response attempt as three observable facts: `obstacle occurred?`, `response used?`, and `started?`.
- The learner distinguishes an obstacle that did not occur, a response that was not used, and a response that was used but did not lead to a start.
- If the response was used but the start missed, the learner returns to the existing earliest-break order: cue, setup, then action.
- The learner chooses at most one next adjustment to the response rule—or deliberately makes no change when the record is unclear.

## Key insight

One attempt need not become a story about motivation: **read the obstacle, response, and start in that order; then inspect the earliest remaining break before changing one thing.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a complete, bounded ritual with a one-obstacle response rule, but consistency remains unproven. This lesson adds interpretation, not new behaviour to execute at the cue. Preserve the primary cue, two-minute action, finish line, one fallback, and one obstacle rule. Do not add a catalogue of contingencies, extra tracking, longer work, catch-up work, or penalties.

## What comes next

Lesson 0016 should teach a compact 30-day progress check: count intentional starts toward the 20-start aim and decide whether to retain the ritual or test one clearly named link, without treating the count as a streak or personal verdict.
