# Learning Record 0022 — Compare One Bounded Test

## Date
2026-08-11

## Lesson
0022 — Compare One Bounded Test

## What was taught

- After one bounded cue, setup, or opening-action test, the learner places one readable pre-test record beside the test record.
- The learner reads the same two facts in each record: `cue occurred?` and `two-minute action started?`.
- The comparison ends with one bounded choice: retain the tested link for two ordinary attempts, return to the prior plan if it clearly added friction, or gather two more records when the evidence is mixed or incomplete.
- One comparison is treated as practical evidence for the next observation, not proof of cause or a reason to upgrade the ritual.

## Key insight

A one-link test stays calm when it is read cautiously: **compare the relevant before-and-after records, choose one modest next move, and keep the rest of the plan stable.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, a four-record check, and a stability boundary, but actual record evidence is unknown. This lesson adds a narrow way to interpret a single test without over-reading it. Preserve the two-minute baseline, modest frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add duration, targets, catch-up work, multiple changes, or extra tracking.

## What comes next

After two ordinary attempts following a retained test or stable plan, teach a compact consolidation decision: identify whether the tested link remains workable enough to keep, or whether the same earliest break again meets the stability boundary for one new bounded test.
