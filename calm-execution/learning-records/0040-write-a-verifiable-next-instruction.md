# Learning Record 0040 — Write a Verifiable Next Instruction

## Date
2026-08-29

## Lesson
0040 — Write a Verifiable Next Instruction

## What was taught

- The learner turns a verified two-record triage result into one exact next instruction.
- Each instruction names one next move and the stable baseline: the current cue, two-minute action, chosen frequency, and any active test.
- Incomplete evidence directs the learner to repeat the same instruction and gather two readable records.
- Mixed or workable evidence directs the learner to retain the plan for two ordinary attempts; a repeated cue or start-side break directs one already-specified matching test.

## Key insight

A next instruction is useful when it can later be checked: **name one next move, name what stays stable, and give it two ordinary attempts.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, rolling two-record triage, and an instruction-fidelity check, while actual evidence remains unknown. This lesson makes the output of triage concrete without adding a new system component. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add duration, catch-up work, multiple changes, or a larger tracking system.

## What comes next

After the next two ordinary attempts, teach a compact instruction audit: compare the exact sentence with both attempts, mark it followed, not followed, or unclear, and repeat it when the evidence cannot yet answer the question.
