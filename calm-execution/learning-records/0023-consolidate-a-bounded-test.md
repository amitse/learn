## Lesson

## What was taught

- The learner reads a bounded test record beside the next two ordinary records using `cue occurred?` then `two-minute action started?`.
- The learner labels each record’s earliest break as none, cue, or start-side.
- Workable, mixed, or incomplete evidence directs the learner to keep the current plan and gather ordinary records.
- Only the same earliest break in at least two readable records supports one new bounded test; a cue break supports one cue test, while a start-side break supports one setup *or* opening-action test.

## Key insight

Consolidation is a decision to preserve clarity: **keep a workable plan; revise only when the same earliest break appears twice, then test one link once.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, stability boundary, and a cautious comparison of one bounded test, but actual records remain unknown. This lesson joins those pieces across three records without treating them as proof. Preserve the two-minute baseline, modest frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add duration, targets, catch-up work, multiple changes, or tracking burden.

## What comes next

After another small set of ordinary attempts, teach a light review that distinguishes a plan that is stable enough to retain from a repeated earliest break that warrants exactly one new bounded test. Keep the review evidence-led and avoid extending the ritual.
