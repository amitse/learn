## Date

## Lesson
0027 — Follow Up on a Bounded Test

## What was taught

- The learner reads the two ordinary attempts after a bounded test by first confirming that the exact named change was used in both.
- For used attempts, the learner reads `cue occurred?` and then `two-minute action started?`.
- Two readable starts without clear added friction support retaining the test for two more ordinary attempts.
- Clear added friction supports returning to the prior plan; unused, mixed, or incomplete evidence supports gathering two ordinary records without a new change.

## Key insight

A tested change earns a modest follow-up, not a verdict: **confirm it was used twice, read cue then start, and choose retention, a clear return, or more ordinary evidence.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, a stability boundary, a one-sentence test specification, and a single-record fidelity check, but actual evidence is unknown. This lesson adds one compact two-record follow-up without increasing duration, frequency, tracking burden, or output expectations. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add catch-up work, multiple changes, or a larger system.

## What comes next

After two more ordinary attempts, teach a compact three-record retention check: distinguish a retained test that remains workable from the same earliest break recurring twice, which may support exactly one new bounded test.
