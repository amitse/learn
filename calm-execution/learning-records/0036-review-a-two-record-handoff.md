# Learning Record 0036 — Review a Two-Record Handoff

## Date
2026-08-25

## Lesson
0036 — Review a Two-Record Handoff

## What was taught

- The learner reads the two ordinary attempts named in a maintenance or evidence handoff in a fixed order: `test used?` (when applicable), `cue occurred?`, then `two-minute action started?`.
- An unused current test or an unclear cue/start field makes the evidence incomplete; the learner keeps the instruction and gathers two ordinary records.
- When neither readable record shows the same earliest break twice, the learner retains the current cue, two-minute action, frequency, and any current test for two more ordinary attempts.
- A cue break repeated twice supports one already-specified cue test; a start-side break repeated twice supports one already-specified setup or opening-action test.

## Key insight

A handoff can stay small after it is used: **read two records in order, then retain the instruction for two more ordinary attempts—or run exactly one supported bounded test.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, a six-record continuity check, and a written two-attempt handoff, while actual evidence remains unknown. This lesson closes that two-attempt loop without increasing duration, frequency, tracking burden, or output expectations. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add catch-up work, multiple changes, or a larger tracking system.

## What comes next

After the next two ordinary attempts, teach a compact rolling review: use the latest two records to retain the current instruction when readable evidence remains mixed or workable, and return to the repeated-break boundary only when the same earliest break appears twice.
