# Learning Record 0029 — Re-triage Four Records

## Date

2026-08-18

## Lesson

0029 — Re-triage Four Records

## What was taught

- The learner reads four recent ordinary records in a fixed order: `test used?` (when applicable), `cue occurred?`, then `two-minute action started?`.
- Each readable record receives one earliest-break label: `none`, `cue`, or `start-side`; an unused test is `incomplete` rather than evidence about the test.
- Working, mixed, or incomplete records direct the learner to retain the current plan for one ordinary attempt.
- The same earliest break in at least two readable records supports exactly one matching bounded test: cue, setup, or opening action.

## Key insight

Four records support a modest decision, not a verdict: **retain a readable plan unless the same earliest break appears twice; then test one matching link and keep the rest stable.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward ritual, a stability boundary, and a three-record retention check, but actual record evidence remains unknown. This lesson reuses the known decision boundary across a four-record sample and makes the next instruction concrete. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add catch-up work, duration targets, multiple changes, or a larger tracking system.

## What comes next

After one ordinary attempt, teach a compact test-fidelity refresh: compare the written test specification with what was actually done, then read cue and start without treating an unused or unclear test as a result.
