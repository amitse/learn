# Learning Record 0019 — Write a Maintenance Rule

## Date
2026-08-08

## Lesson
0019 — Write a Maintenance Rule

## What was taught

- The learner uses two carry-forward records to decide whether the existing cue and two-minute action are working together.
- When both records show `cue occurred? yes` and `two-minute action started? yes`, the learner writes a bounded maintenance rule: keep the cue, action, and frequency unchanged for the next two ordinary attempts.
- A maintenance rule is not an upgrade, claim of permanence, or demand for longer work.
- If either record contains an earliest break, the learner does not force maintenance; they return to the existing one-link review and test at most one adjustment.

## Key insight

Working evidence can support restraint: **when the cue and start worked twice, keep the same small plan for two ordinary attempts before changing anything.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a carry-forward component and a two-record review loop, but new evidence is unknown. This lesson teaches deliberate maintenance only when its two observable conditions are present. It preserves the two-minute baseline, modest frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add duration, targets, extra tracking, catch-up work, or more system components.

## What comes next

After the two maintenance attempts, teach a compact four-record check: retain the plan when cue and start remain workable, or identify one earliest clear break and test one bounded refinement. Keep the review evidence-led and do not increase the baseline.
