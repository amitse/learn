## What was taught

- The learner converts the Lesson 45 audited-pair classification into one exact instruction for the next two ordinary attempts.
- Mixed, workable, or unclear classifications retain the current plan and gather two readable records.
- Two matching readable breaks support only an already-specified matching bounded test; no additional link changes.
- Each instruction names the stable cue and exact two-minute action so the next audit can assess whether it was followed.

## Key insight

A classification becomes useful only when it leads to a checkable next move: **write one restrained instruction, use it for two ordinary attempts, then audit enactment before interpreting outcomes.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner can audit a written instruction and classify a verified pair. This lesson restores the immediate output of that classification without adding another system component. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable boundary. Do not add duration, catch-up work, multiple changes, or more tracking.

## What comes next

After the instruction covers two ordinary attempts, teach a compact audit: compare the exact sentence with both attempts, label each `followed`, `not followed`, or `unclear`, and repeat the instruction when enactment is not yet readable.
