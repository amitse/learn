## Date
2026-10-06

## Lesson
0078 — Classify an Audited Instruction Pair

## What was taught

- Only after both covered attempts are labelled `followed`, the learner reads each record cue-first.
- The learner labels each readable record `cue` (cue did not occur), `start-side` (cue occurred but the exact two-minute action did not start), `none` (both occurred), or `unclear`.
- A new test is warranted only when the same readable earliest break appears twice and an already-specified matching test exists; otherwise the learner retains the instruction and gathers two ordinary records.

## Key insight

Outcome reading is useful only after enactment is confirmed, and the first observable break—not the emotional explanation—is the relevant system data.

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has just audited whether the written two-attempt instruction was enacted. This lesson adds a simple cue-first classification gate while preserving the existing cue, exact two-minute action, frequency, and no-penalty stance. Keep the categories concrete; mixed, successful, and incomplete records call for retention and more readable evidence, not a redesign or catch-up work.

## What comes next

Turn an audit-passed, cue-first classified pair into one exact next instruction: retain and gather two ordinary records, or name the one already-specified matching test, with the observable cue and exact two-minute action included.
