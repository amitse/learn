# Learning Record 0041 — Audit a Two-Attempt Instruction

## Date
2026-08-30

## Lesson
0041 — Audit a Two-Attempt Instruction

## What was taught

- The learner compares the exact prior next-instruction sentence with each of its two covered attempts before interpreting outcomes.
- Each attempt receives one evidence label: `followed`, `not followed`, or `unclear`; unclear fields are not reconstructed from memory.
- If either attempt is not followed or unclear, the learner repeats the same instruction for two ordinary attempts and records it readably.
- Only when both attempts followed the instruction does the learner return to the existing two-record cue/start triage.

## Key insight

An outcome can answer an instruction only when the instruction was enacted: **read the sentence first, label both attempts, then repeat for readable evidence or triage a verified pair.**

## Zone of proximal development notes

The learner has a verifiable next-instruction sentence and a rolling fidelity-and-triage loop, while actual records remain unknown. This lesson practices the immediate next link: auditing the sentence against two attempts. Preserve the two-minute baseline, chosen frequency, no-penalty stance, and one-variable approach. Do not add duration, catch-up work, multiple changes, or a larger tracking system.

## What comes next

After a verified pair, teach a compact cue/start reading drill that distinguishes `cue` from `start-side` without re-auditing the instruction. If either attempt is unverified, retain the audit rule and gather two readable records.
